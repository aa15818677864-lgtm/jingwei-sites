Macau · Retaining Mainland estate counsel

In Macau and Hiring Mainland Estate Counsel: Check These Six Points First

Do not send the family's entire file to an individual contact or sign a blank, all-purpose authority. Before retaining Mainland counsel for an estate, identify six things: the client, the specific Mainland asset, the immediate task, the scope of legal work, the acts that may be delegated, and the records for fees and original documents.

Six points before signing

  • Identify the client and any potential conflict
  • Give each asset a city and immediate task
  • Separate legal scope from delegated acts

Define the people, task, authority, money and documents

A retainer explains which law firm is providing which service, how the work is scoped and how charges are handled. An authority document explains what another person may do before a court, registry, bank or other recipient. Contacting a lawyer is not the same as completing both arrangements.

Identify the client before sharing the family file

List the possible heirs, any person receiving a specific gift, any estate-management role and the relative coordinating communications. A coordinator may collect papers without having authority to retain counsel, disclaim, divide, sell or accept a debt for everyone.

Ask whether the firm would advise one family member or several. If positions already differ, say so before sensitive documents are exchanged so that conflicts can be checked first.

Describe the matter as an asset and an immediate task

Do not stop at “we need help with an inheritance.” Give each Mainland asset its own line, then identify the next task: enquiry, preservation, identifying heirs, preparing a registration, addressing a dispute, sale or collection.

The city, asset and recipient may change the document, attendance and authority requirements. Confirm each item rather than relying on a single promise that everything can be handled remotely.

Make the scope of work measurable

Record the starting point, deliverable and stopping point. The first stage might be an inventory review and missing-document list, a check with one recipient, a defined draft, attendance at one procedure, or work through submission of a particular application.

State whether valuation, tax review, translation, document formalities, litigation, sale or receipt of money is outside that stage. A clear exclusion prevents a family from assuming one engagement covers every later step.

Separate routine authority from outcome-changing decisions

Divide authority into enquiries, filing, receipt of notices, procedural signatures, settlement, disclaimer, sale and receipt of money. For each act, record the signer, recipient, expiry, any power to delegate, any power to dispose of assets or receive money, and whether fresh written approval is required.

Never provide a password, one-time code, banking login or facial authentication. Authority to manage a filing does not automatically include authority to change the final distribution.

Separate legal fees, advances and third-party costs

Confirm the law firm accepting the work, the payee and the formal payment record. Keep translation, searches, travel, valuation and other third-party costs outside one unexplained total. For any advance, set a limit, purpose, approval method and receipt requirement.

Provide copies by stage and log every original

At first contact, provide an index and only the copies needed to describe the matter. Mark each item as complete, missing pages, image only or original not yet found. Ask which identity, family or financial details are needed for the conflict check and which should wait until a retainer exists.

If an original must move, record its name, pages, sender, recipient, date, purpose, return arrangement and tracking number.

Five readers can each take one useful step

First-time caller: prepare a one-page case summary.

Person holding one document: preserve every page and attachment, and note the original's location and intended use.

Older relative: ask who is the client, which asset is involved, what the firm will deliver, who may sign, how charges work and who holds each original.

Family already in disagreement: identify the client and potential conflict before one coordinator promises an outcome for everyone.

Person ready to retain counsel: bring the people sheet, asset-and-task list, deadlines, proposed scope, authority limits, fee questions and originals log.

Use this six-point check before signing

Checklist:
• law firm, responsible lawyer and client;
• asset city, immediate task and receiving body;
• deliverables, stopping point and exclusions;
• delegated acts, expiry, money handling and approval limits;
• legal fees, third-party costs, advances and receipts;
• copies, sensitive data, original delivery and return records.

This article does not decide who may represent the estate or promise that a Macau document will be accepted for a Mainland procedure. It addresses the earlier step: define the people, task, authority, money and documents before the engagement begins.
