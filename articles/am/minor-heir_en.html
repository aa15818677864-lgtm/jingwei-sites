A Minor Is an Heir: What Adults Cannot Decide Alone

When a minor may inherit part of a Mainland China estate, an agreement among the adults is not automatically the child's agreement. Before anyone signs, identify four things: how the child fits into the family tree, what property actually belongs to the estate, who can act for the child, and whether the proposed document reduces, gives up or transfers the child's interest.

Last updated: 2026-07-28

Four points to remember

  • An adult agreement is not the child's agreement
  • Identify the estate before estimating shares
  • Authority to apply is not authority to surrender rights
  • Separate division, sale and control of money

The practical answer

A guardian may handle parts of an application for a child. That does not create an unrestricted power to surrender an inheritance, allocate the property entirely to the adults, sell an asset belonging to the child, or place the child's money under an adult's personal control.

Start with one page divided into four boxes: the minor, the proposed representative, the estate asset and the document waiting for signature. The boxes usually reveal the missing fact faster than a general family discussion.

Map the Child's Place in the Family Before Discussing Percentages

List the deceased's spouse, children and parents, and note anyone who died before the deceased. A minor may be the deceased's child, or may be connected through a parent who has already died. Record the complete relationship chain instead of writing only “grandchild, age 12.”

Give each minor a separate row:

Checklist:
• full name, date of birth and current age;
• relationship to the deceased;
• current parents, carers or guardians;
• available Macau birth, identity and guardianship records;
• each property, account, company interest or other asset that may be involved;
• whether the proposed representative is also an heir who benefits from the plan.

Then identify the estate itself. A flat may already be co-owned. A spouse or another owner may have an interest that must be separated before the deceased's estate can be discussed. A mortgage, management charges and other debts belong on a different schedule. Until that work is done, do not multiply the value of the whole flat by a percentage and call the result the child's confirmed share.

Authority to Apply Is Not Authority to Give the Child's Rights Away

For a Mainland registration or similar procedure, a guardian will commonly be asked to act for the minor and provide evidence of the child's identity, the guardianship relationship and the guardian's identity. Those documents answer who may act. They do not answer whether every proposed decision protects the child.

Pause when a document would:

Checklist:
• renounce all or an undefined part of the estate;
• allocate the entire property to an adult;
• transfer the child's possible interest without value;
• let one relative receive all sale proceeds;
• state that the child will make no future claim.

Some published local inheritance-registration checklists expressly separate an application made for a minor from a renunciation, and state that a guardian cannot renounce the inheritance for the minor. The exact route still depends on the asset, the city and the receiving office. The practical point is simple: do not sign first and investigate the effect later.

If only one document is in front of you, write three questions on its first page:

Checklist:
• What interest of the child changes if this is signed?
• Who receives more property or money as a result?
• Who controls the money afterwards?

An unanswered question is a reason to pause.

Take Extra Care When the Representative Is Also an Heir

A parent or another guardian may also be a co-heir. That does not automatically disqualify the adult from acting, and it does not automatically invalidate every family agreement. It does create a warning sign when the same plan gives the adult more property, money or control while the child receives less.

Ask:

Checklist:
• How does the proposal differ from the child's possible position before the agreement?
• What identifiable benefit does the child receive in exchange for any reduction?
• Is there a valuation, debt schedule, repair need or housing reason supporting the arrangement?
• Where will the child's money be held, who can move it, and what record will remain?

Where the adult signing for the child directly benefits from the proposed allocation, obtain an independent review of the valuation, allocation and representation arrangement. The office handling the matter should also be asked whether another representative, additional evidence or a different procedure is required. A benefiting adult's statement that the plan is “for the child” is not enough by itself.

In a divided family, avoid putting four roles in one person's hands: collecting all information, signing for the child, confirming the facts for the whole family and holding all proceeds. Separating those roles makes a conflict easier to see and the records easier to audit.

Preservation, Registration, Division, Sale and Receipt of Money Are Different Steps

Urgent preservation does not have to wait for the final distribution. A leaking property can be repaired, keys can be secured, rent can be recorded and necessary charges can be tracked. Those acts do not decide who ultimately receives the property.

Registration records the relevant rights. An estate-division agreement allocates particular assets. A sale converts the property into money. Receipt and custody determine who controls the proceeds. Each step needs its own purpose, signatory, supporting documents and money trail.

If someone says, “Let us sell first and sort it out later,” check at least:

Checklist:
• the registered ownership and the part that actually belongs to the estate;
• the minor's possible interest;
• the proposed price, valuation and necessary expenses;
• who will sign the sale and receipt documents;
• the account into which the proceeds will be paid;
• how the child's money will be separately held and recorded.

A buyer's deadline does not remove these questions.

Six Questions Before Sending Macau Documents

Macau birth, identity, death, marriage or guardianship records may connect the child to the deceased and explain the proposed representative's role. A document's name alone does not guarantee that a Mainland recipient will accept it for the intended purpose.

Before releasing originals or signing an authority, ask the actual recipient:

Checklist:
• Is this record needed to prove identity, family relationship, guardianship or authority?
• Is an original, certified copy, translation or other verification required?
• Must all guardians participate?
• Does the child's current age require the child to express a view or sign anything in this procedure?
• Can the step be handled from Macau, who must attend in person, and how specific must the authority be?
• Is the document only for an enquiry or inheritance registration, or does it also cover division, sale or receipt of money?

Record the date, department, contact details and the wording of the answer. A checklist from another city or another family's case may not fit this asset or route.

What Five Different Readers Can Do Today

**Handling an estate for the first time:** draw the family tree before estimating shares.

**Holding only a document that needs signing:** photograph every page and mark any language about renunciation, transfer, receipt of money or no further claim.

**Helping an older relative:** answer four questions first — who is the child, who cares for the child, where is the asset, and who is requesting the signature?

**Facing disagreement in the family:** keep the child's records and money trail separate. Do not let one side alter originals or payment instructions without a record.

**Preparing for legal advice:** bring the family map, the minor and guardian document list, the estate schedule, the document awaiting signature and the proposed allocation.

Stop Before Signing When You See These Warning Signs

Checklist:
• The guardian is asked to surrender all or an undefined inheritance for the child.
• The agreement describes what every adult receives but says nothing about the child's interest or custody of funds.
• The person signing for the child benefits directly from the reduction in the child's position.
• A property sale is proposed without a valuation, price, expense schedule or payment destination.
• Funds are to be paid into a relative's everyday personal account.
• The guardian, the other heirs or the estate assets are still uncertain.
• The recipient demands a signature but will not explain the document's purpose or next step.

The safer sequence is to identify the child and representative, verify the estate and the document, and only then discuss division or sale. Adults can reach an agreement quickly. Protecting the child's position requires a clear record of authority, benefit and control of money.
