A Mainland Bank Asked for More Inheritance Documents. How Should the Family Respond?

A request for additional documents does not necessarily mean the application has been rejected. The family should learn what is missing, which fact it proves, and what form the bank accepts. A 2024 notice requires a bank to identify relevant requirements in one communication when materials are incomplete or non-compliant.

Published: 2026-10-23

Request an itemised explanation

Ask the bank to identify each missing document, the point to correct, the required form, and the submission method. Ask whether each item proves death, family relationship, the full set of heirs, a will, or authority for one person to act for another. If the only response is “incomplete,” request enough detail to prepare a useful submission rather than guessing and resending the same records.

The notice requires one notification of relevant requirements in this situation. It does not mean a generic checklist fits every branch, product, or family. A bank may still clarify inconsistencies during review. Record the date, channel, branch, and department, and retain letters, emails, text messages, and receipts.

Separate routine correction from a succession dispute

A missing identity document, proof of death, family record, or acceptable format may be a routine correction. For a Macao-based family dealing with a Mainland deposit, first ask the receiving bank what it requires. If a Mainland-use notarial document is needed, confirm whether it should be issued by a China-Appointed Attesting Officer (Macao) and verified by China Legal Services (Macao), or handled directly by that company. The correct form depends on what must be proved and the receiving institution.

Questions about a will’s validity, whether all heirs have been identified, whether someone renounced, or whether one person may collect for another may go beyond a simple correction. Organise will versions, death and family records, the positions of other heirs, and existing notarial or court documents. Then consider an agreement or court route. The bank reviews its documents; it does not decide a disputed inheritance share.

Keep proof of each submission

Prepare items one by one and label each copy with its name, number, and the requirement it addresses. For in-person delivery, request a receipt. For mail or online submission, retain the tracking record or upload confirmation. If the bank inspects an original, ask whether it will return it immediately and keep a complete copy at home.

Use the receipt number to ask which items were accepted, who handles the next step, and whether other heirs must participate. If names, dates, or relationship details differ, explain the discrepancy to the bank and confirm the accepted proof. File documents in the bank’s requested order and keep an identical copy.
