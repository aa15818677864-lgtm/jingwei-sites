Preserve verifiable material, then ask a focused Mainland question

When a Macau family finds accounts, cards, messages and transfers, it should not treat one notice, message or copy as a final conclusion. It may identify a Mainland asset, contract, account or process, but the family must separately check the person's identity, current authority, asset position and the receiving requirements.

A useful first step is one working sheet showing each item, its source, date, original location and known gap. This reduces the risk of losing context or taking over an account, signing, or distributing anything before the facts are clear.

1. Preserve the source and method of access

Keep the original screen, both sides, attachment, envelope or notice time for accounts, cards, messages and transfers. Record who supplied it, when it was obtained and whether it was forwarded. A cropped screenshot or one copied sentence may omit the context that matters.

Where an item concerns another person's account, device, company records or private messages, use only material lawfully held by the family. Do not guess passwords, bypass verification or act in the deceased person's name; first confirm who is authorised to ask for more.

2. Separate people, assets and chronology

Use one column for people and roles, one for the Mainland city, asset, company or contract clue, and one for dates and sources. A document carrying the deceased person's name does not itself prove entitlement or authority to act.

If names, addresses, figures or dates conflict, mark them for checking rather than correcting them by assumption. Keep Macau death, family and heirship materials as a separate file; an asset clue cannot replace that identity work.

3. Ask one focused Mainland question

Tell the Mainland receiving party, local asset contact point or instructed lawyer what was found (accounts, cards, messages and transfers), the city involved, who may represent the estate and the precise issue to confirm. That is more useful than asking only whether the matter can be handled.

Requirements can differ by asset type, city, dispute status and applicant identity. Until a receiving side gives a clear response, no one should promise recovery, transfer, collection or any other result.

4. Keep the next step in a checklist

Separate originals held, copies that may be obtainable, people to verify, institutions to contact and notices with deadlines. Record each handover or transmission by date, recipient and purpose, sharing sensitive material only as needed.

If relatives disagree, material may have been altered, or a demand or court deadline exists, preserve the notice and source record in full and promptly identify the appropriate Mainland China route.
