You Have a Macau Heirship Document: What Will the Mainland Bank Still Check?

Do not visit branches at random with a thick file. Choose one family contact, organise the account clues and ask the bank three focused questions.

Ask three things on the first call

  • Which family member may apply?
  • Which branch or team will handle it?
  • Is this an account enquiry or a withdrawal?

The document matters, but it is not a withdrawal pass

A Macau heirship document can help the bank understand the relationship between the deceased and the heirs. The bank must still identify the account and product, confirm who is applying, and decide whether the family is locating an account, requesting available information or collecting the funds.

Start with the bank name, account-opening city and the last few digits of any card or account number. One enquiry call does not mean the bank has agreed to disclose a balance or release money.

1. Separate account tracing, information requests and collection

If the family has only a bank card, text message or passbook photo, record the clue first. Locating an account, asking what information can be released and collecting the balance are separate steps. The eligible applicant and required papers may differ at each step.

2. Put the account clues on one page

List the bank, last digits, account-opening city, known product and the date of the latest record. Keep ordinary deposits, fixed deposits, investment products and joint accounts separate. Knowing the bank does not mean every branch can handle the request.

3. Keep the people file separate from the account file

The people file contains the death record, Macau heirship document, applicant identification and any name-change evidence. The account file contains cards, passbooks, messages, transaction receipts and product names. The heirship document does not establish every balance, product status or competing account holder.

A simpler route may exist for some smaller deposits, but it still depends on the applicant, product and absence of a dispute. Do not organise the whole family around a threshold found in an online post.

4. Use a plain first-call script

Say: ‘A family member has died. We have a Macau heirship document and a clue to an account with your bank. Who should apply, which branch handles the matter, and what should we submit first?’ Also ask whether an authorised person can make the initial enquiry, which originals are needed and how authority and identity will be checked.
