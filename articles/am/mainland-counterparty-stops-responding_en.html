Mainland Counterparty Silent? What a Macau Company Should Preserve

When a Mainland counterparty suddenly stops responding, do not spend the first few days sending the same demand to the same person. Do not assume that the company has disappeared either. First work out whether the silence is limited to one salesperson, a project group or every established company channel. At the same time, preserve the original communications, payment details, transaction records and contact attempts before accounts, devices or instructions change.

Last updated: 2026-07-27

Four points to remember

  • One silent contact does not mean the company has disappeared
  • Preserve source accounts, devices, messages and payment instructions
  • Verify every new company, account and contact independently
  • Public information is a lead, not proof of debt or assets

The practical answer

The aim is to create a record that can still be checked later. This does not guarantee recovery, and a change in public company information does not prove what happened to the debt. It gives counsel a cleaner starting point for deciding whether the problem is a broken line of communication or a wider issue involving the contracting entity, authority, payment instructions or timing.

This article starts after normal communication has broken down. If the counterparty still replies but keeps postponing payment, the first job is usually to organise the contract, performance, payments, reconciliations and collection history into a transaction timeline. That is a different task.

The short version is simple: preserve the source records, identify who has actually gone silent, and only then decide whether the matter needs to be escalated.

Who has actually stopped responding?

List the Mainland company named in the contract, the signatory, salesperson, project lead and finance contact. Add the email addresses, telephone numbers, group chats and company accounts that the parties normally used. One employee failing to answer is not the same as the contracting company becoming unreachable.

Most situations initially fall into one of four groups:

Checklist:
• One salesperson is silent, but the company's telephone, email or other employees remain reachable.
• The usual contacts are silent, but established company channels still exist and no replacement contact has been confirmed.
• The main company channels no longer work, while public information also shows a change in address, responsible person or status.
• A new contact appears and asks for payment to a different company, a personal account or a new bank account.

The records needed may overlap, but the risks do not. The fourth situation deserves particular care. A message posted in an old group chat is not, by itself, enough reason to change payment instructions before identity and authority have been checked.

Six steps for the first 30 minutes

Checklist:
• Pause deletion of chats, emails, voice messages, attachments and call records. If a business device must be replaced, reset or returned, make a complete backup first and record who handled it, when and how.
• Capture the current account names, telephone numbers, email addresses, group members and last reply times, while keeping the original accounts and full conversation history available.
• Find the contracting company's full name, registration details, address, signatory and contractual notice method.
• Preserve the latest payment, reconciliation, repayment promise, account-change instruction and dispute response in full.
• Start a contact log recording the date, channel, recipient, message sent, system result and whether anyone replied.
• Appoint one person to hold the source material. Everyone else should work from copies so files are not renamed, cropped or rearranged by several people at once.

On a phone, each contact attempt can be recorded as six short lines. There is no need to wait for a perfect spreadsheet. Preserve what happened first; consolidate it later.

Give each person one clear handoff

Checklist:
• The business owner appoints the record custodian, decides whether new payments or account changes should pause, and avoids publicly labelling the counterparty before the facts are checked.
• Finance provides bank statements, payment receipts, reconciliations, the outstanding-balance calculation and every instruction to change the receiving account.
• Sales or project staff provide complete chats, emails, attachments, voice messages, the last normal reply and the actual role of each contact.
• The device or account custodian explains where the source data sits, whether the account is still accessible, and what exports, backups or device changes have already occurred.
• The person arranging the first legal consultation brings a one-page silence triage, the latest transaction material and the source record most at risk of being lost. They do not need to read every file first.

Each person should supply the records or clues they actually hold. One colleague should not recreate another colleague's history from memory. The owner needs three answers: who is silent, what may disappear, and which decision can no longer wait.

Record the company and each contact separately

Copy the full company name from the contract, orders, invoices and bank records. Do not rely on a brand, shop name or chat nickname. For each individual, record the name, title, account, company email used and what that person actually did in the transaction.

If public registration or status information is available, preserve the complete result and the date of the search. Note changes in the company name, address, responsible person or status. Public information can have scope and timing limits. It is a verification lead, not standalone proof of the debt, a person's authority, the company's operating location, its assets or the prospects of recovery.

Also separate the company named in the contract from the delivery recipient, invoice addressee, payer and day-to-day contact. Similar company names do not make several entities a single counterparty.

Keep the full communication history around the silence

Screenshots are useful working copies, but they should not replace an available source chat. Retain the account details, surrounding conversation, attachments, voice messages, images, timestamps and group members. For email, retain the original message, sender and recipient addresses, attachments and reply chain.

When data is exported, record who performed the export, when, from which device and account, and where the source remains. Do not select only helpful lines or splice together messages from different dates, entities or contacts.

Record non-response in neutral terms. For example: "Reconciliation sent to the established company email on 20 July; no system bounce observed; no reply by 23 July." The lack of a bounce only describes what the system showed at that time. It does not prove that a particular person received or read the message, or that any formal notice requirement was satisfied.

Isolate every payment-account change

For each payment already made, retain the payer, recipient, account, date, amount, currency and bank reference. If the counterparty asked to change the receiving account, use a different company, accept payment through an individual or move an old balance to another project, keep that instruction together with the sending account, time and verification steps.

A new contact may know details of the transaction without having authority to represent the original company. A small test payment is not a substitute for checking identity and authority. Verify new instructions through a company channel that your business already held independently, keep any written basis supplied, and do not follow the new payment instruction until it has been checked.

Connect the silence to a specific transaction

This is not a second version of the full debt timeline. Its purpose is to connect each break in communication to the relevant transaction. Record the contract or order, the delivery or service, the balance concerned, the latest dispute and the capacity in which the last person replied. Whether that person had authority to speak for the company remains a separate question.

Contracts, delivery or service records, acceptance or objections, invoices, reconciliations, bank records and repayment proposals still matter. But one invoice or one message saying "payment next month" is not the whole transaction. If records are split between a Macau owner, finance staff and a Mainland project colleague, first identify who holds each source file. Do not rush to reformat everything.

When waiting may create a larger problem

Prompt legal review is sensible when any of the following appears:

Checklist:
• The established company channels all stop working while the address, responsible person or public status also changes.
• A new company, personal account or unfamiliar contact is introduced without a clear basis of authority.
• Only screenshots remain and access to the source account, device, attachments or full conversation may soon be lost.
• Just before going silent, the counterparty denies performance, claims a large deduction or says the contracting entity was someone else.
• Someone asks for old messages to be deleted, documents to be backdated, or transactions involving several companies to be merged into one confirmation.
• Contractual notice, timing or dispute terms have not been reviewed and further delay may affect the available choices.

The fact that one company is in Macau and another is in the Mainland does not, on its own, decide how formal notice should be given or which procedure applies. These are signals to investigate. They do not establish fraud, closure, asset movement or that recovery is impossible.

Five things not to do

Do not publish unverified accusations. Do not access an account without authority. Do not collect large amounts of personal information from unrelated relatives or employees. Do not recreate service, reply or collection dates. Do not rely on a cropped screenshot as the only source record.

Collect only what is connected to the transaction, communication and verification purpose, and limit internal access. Ordinary screenshots and exports can support initial organisation, but they should not be described as a formal evidence method suitable for every matter. Later technical preservation does not automatically prove that the original message was true, that its sender could bind the company or that the debt was undisputed.

Take a one-page silence triage to the first consultation

Write these seven items vertically so they remain readable on a phone:

Checklist:
• The company named in the contract.
• The last normal contact and that person's role.
• The date of the last actual reply.
• Established company channels that still work.
• Changes found in public information and the search date.
• Any new account, company or contact instruction.
• The device or account holding the source electronic records.

Attach three sets of material: the complete communications directly connected to the silence, the latest transaction and balance records, and every instruction changing the account or entity. Then state three questions: Who has actually gone silent? Which source record is most likely to disappear? Which notice, timing or response decision needs to be checked first?

Once those questions are clear, it becomes easier to decide whether to verify more information, send a formal communication, preserve a particular record or assess another dispute-resolution step.
