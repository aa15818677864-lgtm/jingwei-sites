A Family Member Was Left Out of a Macau Heirship Document: What Should You Do?

Do not type a name into the scanned copy or keep using the document as though the family list were complete.

Last updated: 2026-07-25

Do these three things first

  • Pause any further use of the document
  • Preserve what has already been filed
  • Take the new facts back to the issuing office

Pause first, then give both sides the same facts

The document may already be in the family file when an old birth record reveals another child, or descendants of a child who died years ago. The immediate question is not whom to blame. It is how to stop an incomplete family picture from travelling further through the case.

A prompt disclosure does not necessarily mean the earlier work was wrong. It gives the Macau office and the Mainland recipient a chance to assess the same updated facts before anyone relies on the document again.

1. Record how far the document has travelled

Do not send the document to another institution or type a new name into a scanned copy. Record whether it remains at home, has been sent to a Mainland lawyer, has reached a property or bank desk, or has already been used alongside a family distribution document.

The response may depend on that stage. If a Mainland desk has accepted the file, keep the receipt, case number and latest correspondence. The point is to preserve the trail before asking how the document should be corrected or replaced.

2. Identify the missing person before discussing shares

Write down the person's name, birth details, relationship to the deceased, whether the person is living and where the supporting record came from. The phrase 'there was another family member' is not enough to establish that person's place in the estate.

Dates matter. If the person died before the deceased, record that date and identify any descendants. If the person survived the deceased but later died, a second estate may be involved. Do not assume that a spouse belongs in either chain without checking the timeline and the applicable documents.

3. Return to the Macau office, then notify the Mainland recipient

Take the original document and application papers, the newly found relationship record, a short discovery timeline and a note of the document's intended Mainland use. The issuing office can then decide whether the original statement requires correction, supplementation or a fresh process.

If a Mainland lawyer or desk already holds the document, explain that another potentially relevant relative has been identified and that the Macau document is under review. Report the facts and current stage. Do not promise a final share or ask the recipient to proceed unchanged.

4. Family agreement is not a substitute for a complete family picture

An agreement can record what the family wants to do, but it cannot make an omitted person disappear. First establish who must participate. Distribution, renunciation, authority and sale decisions come afterwards.

For now, preserve the original file and submission trail, prepare one identity-and-relationship sheet for the newly found person, and list the Mainland asset, city and receiving institution. Do not alter or conceal the document gap.
