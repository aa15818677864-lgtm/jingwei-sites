Macau Company Chasing a Mainland Receivable? Build the Timeline First

When a Mainland business partner keeps delaying payment, do not begin by dropping every file into one folder or asking whether a single invoice is enough. Start by putting the parties, agreement, delivery, acceptance, invoicing, payments, reconciliations and collection messages on one timeline. The aim is to identify which amount fell due, what objections were raised and where the record still has a gap.

Four points to remember

  • Identify the transaction and the actual company parties
  • Separate the agreement from delivery and acceptance records
  • Match invoices, reconciliations and bank entries one by one
  • Preserve original collection messages with their context

The practical answer

An invoice alone will not usually establish that goods were delivered, services were completed, work was accepted or the balance is undisputed. The reverse is also true: an imperfect contract does not make every other transaction record useless. Once each item is tied to a date and a purpose, a lawyer can more readily decide whether the next step is to fill an evidence gap, confirm the correct company, make a formal payment demand or assess another dispute route.

First identify the transaction. It may be a sale of goods, a continuing supply arrangement, a service engagement, a project, a loan or a mix of several arrangements. The timeline below is an organising method, not a single legal test for delivery, acceptance, payment or disputes across every contract type.

If You Have 20 Minutes, Find These Four Items

Begin with the contract or purchase order, the latest reconciliation or balance calculation, bank records showing actual payments, and the other side's latest complete reply about payment. Put those four items together, then assign named colleagues to find delivery records, acceptance records and earlier communications.

Do not wait for the owner, finance team, sales team and project team to produce a complete file. Record what is already known. Mark missing items as pending, name the person responsible and set a target date.

Start on a Phone, Then Expand Each Event into Eight Fields

For every event, record eight points: date, the companies involved, what happened, the amount, the supporting document, who holds that document, what the other side said at the time, and what remains to be checked. If an eight-column sheet is awkward on a phone, write the same fields vertically and let one colleague consolidate them later.

Keep one event on each row. For example: contract signed on 3 April; goods delivered on 18 April; receipt signed on 20 April; invoice issued on 5 May; payment due on 31 May; part payment received on 10 June. Do not compress several months into the sentence, "They have always delayed payment."

Step One: Confirm Which Two Companies Actually Traded

Copy the full names of the Macau company and the Mainland counterparty from the original documents. Do not substitute a brand, a chat-group nickname or a salesperson's name. The buyer under the contract, delivery recipient, invoice addressee, actual payer and everyday contact may be different entities. Similar names do not prove they are the same company.

List who signed, ordered, received, accepted, invoiced, paid and promised payment. If a company name, project company, payment account or receiving account changed, preserve documents from before and after the change. Do not combine several entities into a single label such as "the customer."

Also record the job title, company email address, company account or seal used by anyone who signed or replied. Participation by sales, project or finance staff does not automatically mean that each person could confirm the whole balance, extend a deadline or agree to a repayment arrangement. Preserve the original exchange before deciding what it establishes.

Step Two: Separate the Agreement from What Happened

Contracts, quotations, orders, addenda and confirmation emails show what the parties said should happen. Delivery notes, service reports, acceptance records, project messages and return records show what happened in practice. Review the two groups side by side; neither automatically replaces the other.

The timeline should identify the goods or project, quantity, price, currency, delivery method, acceptance arrangement, payment terms, notice provisions and dispute terms. Record any complaint about quality, quantity, delay, set-off or returns on the date it was made. A file containing only favourable material may produce a balance that does not match the transaction history.

If the contract mentions a court, arbitration, notice address or delivery method, copy the wording accurately. Do not decide where a dispute belongs merely because one party is in Macau and the other is in Mainland China. Who sends a formal notice, where it goes, which language is used and what procedure may follow all require review of the complete terms and actual performance.

Step Three: Keep Invoices, Reconciliations and the Claimed Balance Separate

For each invoice, record the project, date, currency, amount and how it was delivered to the other side. Link it to the relevant order, delivery or service record. An invoice can form part of the transaction record, but it does not replace proof of delivery, acceptance or the unpaid balance.

Preserve the complete version and context of reconciliations, balance confirmations, repayment plans and messages such as "payment will be arranged next month." Check which amounts the message covers, whether returns or deductions were included, who sent it and whether it changed the original payment arrangement. Do not save only one sentence that appears to acknowledge a debt.

Step Four: Match Every Bank Entry

For each payment, record the payer, recipient, date, amount, currency, bank reference and account used. Then match it to an invoice or project. Separate part payments, combined payments, third-party payments, refunds and bank charges.

If finance only has a month-end total, return to the bank statement, remittance advice and accounting entry. The unpaid balance should be traceable from the original amount through payments, refunds, discounts and supported deductions. It should not depend on a colleague's memory.

Step Five: Preserve Collection Messages in Context

Emails, chats, voice messages, demand letters and call notes may show how payment was discussed. Keep the original message, attachments, sending account, date and surrounding conversation. A screenshot is useful as a working copy, but it should not replace an available original chat or email.

Tie each request for payment to a specific contract, invoice, delivery date, reconciled balance and requested response date. A message saying only "please pay as soon as possible" may later be difficult to match to a particular amount. If the counterparty mentions cash-flow pressure, pending acceptance, a set-off or another objection, record its words and date without merging different replies into one explanation.

Pause Routine Collection When These Warning Signs Appear

Pause and check the file if the contracting company differs from the recipient; the counterparty suddenly uses a new company or personal account; delivery, acceptance or service completion has no matching record; a new quality or set-off complaint appears; the identity of the person promising payment is unclear; or the company has stopped operating, faces multiple proceedings or shows another risk signal that needs verification.

These signs do not prove that recovery is impossible, and they do not justify an immediate remedy by themselves. They mean the evidence, company identities and risk should be checked separately before the next step is chosen.

Give Your Lawyer Three Working Documents

For the first review, prepare an event timeline, a document index numbered to match it, and a one-page balance calculation showing the original amount, payments, refunds, discounts, disputed deductions and the balance claimed.

Add three questions: Which link in the record is weakest? Which amounts are affected by the counterparty's objections? Should the next step be to confirm the correct entity, collect a missing document, send a formal request or assess another procedure? Getting those answers first is more useful than sending a strongly worded letter before the amount and supporting record line up.
