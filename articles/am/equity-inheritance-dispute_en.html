What Mainland Share Evidence Should a Macau Family Preserve?

One shareholder's message is not the company record. Preserve the ownership trail, a properly delivered request and the Macau estate papers separately.

Preserve the trail before arguing about ownership

A Macau family can take three useful steps immediately: preserve every company record already available, send the company a focused written request, and organise the Macau documents that may establish estate authority. A missing name in public information does not prove that no interest exists, and one shareholder cannot speak for the company's records without authority.

The company material addresses the deceased person's interest. The Macau papers address who may act for the estate. Delivery and response records show what the company and individual shareholders actually did. These evidence trails must eventually meet, but none replaces another.

1. Preserve the company material available now

Record the full company name, registration identifier, address, the deceased person's current and former names, and any known contribution or role. Save complete public-information pages with the query date rather than clipping one shareholder name.

Collect investment papers, payment records, contribution receipts, dividend transfers, emails, old business cards and meeting material. Arrange originals and images by date. Participation in the business is a lead; it is not proof of a particular percentage.

2. Separate the register, articles and Macau estate papers

A shareholder register may show names, contributions and qualification dates. The company articles may contain a separate arrangement for succession to shareholder status. An undated image without complete pages should be recorded as a partial source, not treated as the full register.

Macau heir or estate papers may help establish who can act, but they do not prove how many Mainland shares existed. A name in the company register does not establish the death, family relationship or estate authority needed for the next step.

3. Send a focused request to the company

Asking one shareholder to help is a personal conversation. It does not show that the company received a formal request. Identify the requester, the estate documents currently available, the records and dates sought, and the person or channel expected to respond.

Different access conditions may apply to articles, registers, resolutions, financial reports, books and vouchers. A request for books or vouchers should state a specific purpose and use the proper applicant status. Record separately whether an individual stayed silent, the company did not answer, the company refused, or someone claimed that a record did not exist.

4. Do not take control of accounts, seals or books

Concern about missing evidence does not justify logging into the deceased person's account, removing a company seal, signing for the company or obtaining books privately from staff. Preserve material the family lawfully holds, then establish who may request company information.

Use a three-column sheet: ownership clues, estate authority, and the request-and-response timeline. If the company appears to be changing or disposing of the interest, give the dated material to a professional promptly so the available options can be assessed from the actual evidence.
