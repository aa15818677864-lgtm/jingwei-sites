Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

Mainland properties in several cities: a practical registration sequence for Hong Kong heirs

When estate homes sit in Shenzhen, Guangzhou and other cities, families often hope to file everything at once. Each property’s local registry requirements should be checked separately. A shared family-evidence file helps, but does not make every city’s filing list identical.

Published: 2026-10-24

Make a property-by-city inventory

For each home, record its location, registered owner, co-ownership, mortgage or seizure, use and tenancy. Keep old certificates, contracts and registry searches together so that similar addresses are not confused.

Confirm the receiving office and route for each property

Ask each local registry about inheritance registration, whether notarisation or a final court document is required, who must attend, and how Hong Kong declarations or powers of attorney are accepted. A city-level online or one-stop service does not necessarily cover another city.

Reuse core evidence, but keep separate filing records

Build one clear file of death, family, marriage and will evidence, then prepare originals or certified copies as each registry requires. Keep each application, deficiency notice and receipt. After registration, verify the names, shares and restrictions on every new certificate.

Documents and points to check

  • City, address and unit identifier for each property.
  • Each registry’s contact and written document list.
  • Identity, relationship and authority documents for every heir.
  • Application, deficiency and receipt references for each city.
  • Post-registration checks of title shares and encumbrances.
