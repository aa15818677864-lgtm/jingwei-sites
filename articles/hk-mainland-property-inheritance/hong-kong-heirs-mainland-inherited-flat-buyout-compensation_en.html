Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

Can one heir keep a Mainland China inherited flat and compensate the others?

A flat cannot usually be divided like a bank balance. The heirs may consider a buyout with compensation or continued co-ownership. Before pricing either option, identify the deceased's actual interest in the Mainland China property and any rights held by others.

Published: 2026-11-17

Identify the estate interest first

The full market value is not necessarily the value of the estate. The property may have belonged partly to a spouse or another co-owner before death. Review the title record, acquisition documents and relevant agreements before calculating any inheritance allocation.

Check the will, the potential heirs and any disputed or renounced claims. Occupation of the flat or payment of routine expenses does not, by itself, establish an inheritance share.

Make the compensation proposal specific

Article 1156 of the PRC Civil Code permits valuation, appropriate compensation or co-ownership for estate property unsuitable for physical division. It does not guarantee that any one heir can unilaterally take the flat. The available solution depends on the rights and evidence in the particular matter.

Record the valuation date and method, the interest being acquired, payment terms, and the proposed registration sequence. Check mortgages, tenancies, occupation and any other restrictions. A vague agreement to settle the price later may leave the central dispute unresolved.

Connect the family agreement to Mainland registration

Hong Kong based heirs should assemble title, death, relationship and will documents. If someone will act for them in Mainland China, confirm the receiving authority's current requirements for identity and authorization documents.

If the heirs disagree, isolate whether the dispute concerns estate ownership, inheritance status, valuation or payment. A private understanding should not be treated as a completed transfer; the property authority's registration requirements must still be met.

Documents and decisions to prepare

  • Title and existing co-ownership records
  • Evidence of the deceased's interest
  • Will and potential heirs
  • Valuation basis and date
  • Mortgage, tenancy and occupation status
  • Payment and registration sequence
