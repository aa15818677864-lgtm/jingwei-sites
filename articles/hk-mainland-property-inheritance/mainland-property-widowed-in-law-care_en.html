Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

A widowed daughter-in-law cared for a Mainland parent: what should Hong Kong relatives check?

After her spouse died, a daughter-in-law may have continued to support the spouse's parents. Whether she participates in the inheritance of their Mainland property depends on the actual support she provided, not merely the family label. Organise the records before presenting the question to the institution where the property is located.

Published: 2026-11-17

Separate ordinary in-law status from the statutory exception

An in-law relationship alone does not normally make a daughter-in-law a statutory heir of her spouse's parents. Article 1129 of the Mainland Civil Code provides an exception for a widowed daughter-in-law or son-in-law who provided the principal support to the deceased parent-in-law. A former household entry or a general statement that she helped is not a final answer.

The Supreme People's Court interpretation refers to principal economic support or substantial assistance through labour. Financial contributions, daily care and medical support may all be relevant. Occasional visits or transfers should not be described as sustained principal support without the underlying facts.

Build a dated record of care

Map the spouse's death, the older person's needs, and the period of financial and practical support. Preserve original transfer records, care and medical documents, living arrangements and other available evidence, with their issuers and dates. Do not create a retrospective narrative that the records cannot support.

Ask the Mainland registry or notary handling the property what form of Hong Kong documents and representative authority it accepts. The family's Hong Kong residence affects document handling; the property issue remains a Mainland matter.

Review her position separately from her children's

The widowed in-law's potential first-order status turns on her own support. A child's possible inheritance by representation depends on a separate family and death timeline. The Supreme People's Court interpretation states that remarriage does not itself remove the widowed in-law's Article 1129 position, and that this position does not prevent a child's representation claim.

Before filing, identify the other potential heirs, any will or renunciation and the property's ownership position. If relatives dispute the support history, preserve both sides' records and establish the appropriate Mainland dispute process before anyone signs a final division statement for the family.

Documents to gather

  • Death records and dates for the spouse and parent-in-law.
  • Marriage, identity and family relationship records.
  • Original financial and practical-care evidence.
  • Property ownership and will documents.
  • Other heirs and any representation claim.
  • Disputed facts and local filing requirements.
