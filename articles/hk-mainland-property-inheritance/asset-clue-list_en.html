How to Build a Searchable Asset Clue List for a Relative's Mainland Estate

Some families know that a parent or grandparent probably had property or savings in Mainland China, but cannot find the title document or bank card.

Last updated: 2026-07-23

This may be close to what your family is facing

  • Record more than the name of the asset
  • Separate what was seen from what was only heard
  • Start with the highest-value clues

Start with the situation itself

Some families know that a parent or grandparent probably had property or savings in Mainland China, but cannot find the title document or bank card. Asking every possible institution is rarely a practical first step. The better starting point is to turn scattered memories into clues that can be checked.

Record more than the name of the asset

A note that says only “Guangzhou flat, bank savings, company shares” is not enough. Add at least three fields to each item: where the information came from, when it relates to, and who currently holds the relevant record.

For property, record the address or village name, approximate purchase year, former occupants, key holder, and any contract, payment, mortgage or rent record. For a bank account, note the bank, branch city, last digits of a card, message screenshots, deposit slips and anyone who attended the original appointment. For a company, record its full name, operating city, known partners, dividends and tax or accounting records.

Separate what was seen from what was only heard

Family recollection can quickly become confused with confirmed fact. Use three simple labels: supported by a record, personally seen by someone, or mentioned only in conversation. This helps decide which clue deserves an early search and which one first needs supporting material.

Also record who controls the asset or information now. Who lives in or lets the property? Who has the bank card? Who continues to operate the company? Control does not decide ownership, but it can affect the order in which records and risks should be checked.

Start with the highest-value clues

Prioritise assets with a clear location or institution, continuing income, or a realistic risk of disposal. A vague story with no city, address or institution can remain in a second-round list.

Once the list is organised, separate the enquiries into property, banking, employment benefits, company interests and other assets. The point of the list is to give each enquiry a concrete starting place, not to send family members from city to city without a plan.
