The Old Property Was Demolished: How Can a Hong Kong Family Trace Compensation or a Replacement Home?

Demolition does not erase the paper trail. First establish what the old property became: cash, replacement housing, transition payments or an entitlement that has not yet been delivered.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Reconnect the old address to the project
  • Separate agreements, payments and housing
  • Use a timeline to locate the missing step

Identify the present form of the entitlement before dividing it

Cash compensation, replacement housing, transition payments and an uncompleted entitlement may all originate from the same old home, but they are not the same asset. The family should first connect the property, the project and the surviving entitlement.

Once the project, original registrant, agreement signatory and performance status are clear, the family can decide whether it is tracing money, a home or a dispute.

Reconnect the address to the demolition project

Record every version of the old address, the village or estate name, nearby landmarks, former occupants and the approximate relocation year. Arrange receipts, bills, photographs, letters and notices by date.

Replace the vague statement that the property was demolished years ago with three questions: which project, who was recorded and was an agreement signed? These clues usually lead to the right file.

Find out what “there was a replacement home” really means

An expression of interest, an allocated unit, physical delivery and a home ready for registration are different stages. Look for selection records, delivery notices, handover records, management bills and registration papers.

If a relative has occupied or rented the unit, record when that began and who handled income and expenses. Occupation is important evidence, but it does not settle every family entitlement.

Keep four names in separate columns

List the old property holder, the compensation agreement signatory, the payment recipient and the present registrant or occupant of the replacement home. If the names differ, trace how each step occurred.

Signing, collecting or managing on behalf of the family shows participation in the process. It does not by itself prove that the person received the whole entitlement personally.

Build a timeline and look for gaps

Place acquisition, the demolition notice, the agreement, unit selection or payment, the death, handover and registration on one line. Attach one verifiable document or transaction to each date.

A selected unit with no handover record, or a reported payment with no amount or destination account, identifies the part that needs further inquiry.

Preserve records if someone has already received or dealt with the asset

Keep complete agreements, bank records, receipts, property-management material and messages. Until the facts are clear, avoid changing locks, collecting rent, signing a waiver or directing the entire entitlement to one person.

A useful first file contains the old address and project year, every project or agreement number, and a list of who signed, received money or used the replacement home.
