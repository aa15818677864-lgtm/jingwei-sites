Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

A parking space linked to a Mainland home: distinguish ownership from use rights

A family may refer to a home and its parking space as one package, but they may represent different rights. Check for separate registration and purchase documents, or whether the arrangement is a lease, civil-defence space or long-term use contract, before listing it as estate property.

Published: 2026-10-23

Locate the space number and separate title records

Check for separate real estate registration, a sale or gift contract, payment records and invoices. A property agreement listing an allocated space, parking card or fee receipt may not prove ownership.

The contract determines whether a title transfer is available

The Civil Code allows planned parking spaces to be allocated by sale, gift or lease. A civil-defence or common space, or a use-only arrangement, may follow different transfer rules. Check the term, management conditions and local registration procedure.

List the home and parking interest separately

Use separate entries for the home, owned space, contractual use interest and unpaid fees. If the space is registered to the deceased, check the local inheritance process. For a contractual right, ask the developer or manager about succession, refunds and the remaining term.

Documents and points to check

  • Space number, location and separate title entry.
  • Sale, gift, lease or civil-defence-space agreement.
  • Payments, management fees and remaining term.
  • Any co-ownership or transfer restriction.
  • Responses from the registry, manager or contract counterparty.
