Heirs in Hong Kong, Mainland China and Overseas: How to Move a Mainland Estate Forward

When heirs live in different places, the first question is usually whether everyone must travel to Mainland China.

Last updated: 2026-07-23

This may be close to what your family is facing

  • Do not appoint one representative before mapping the matter
  • Build a complete heir and location table
  • Group people by the action they can take

Start with the situation itself

When heirs live in different places, the first question is usually whether everyone must travel to Mainland China. Often that is not the right starting point. First identify who can cooperate, who can sign remotely, who is temporarily unavailable, and who actively disagrees or cannot be found.

Distance alone does not stop an estate matter. Problems are more often caused by an incomplete heir list, a power of attorney that is too vague, informal agreement without signatures, or different document requirements at the institution handling the asset.

Do not appoint one representative before mapping the matter

It may be sensible for one relative or a Mainland lawyer to coordinate the work, but first identify the assets, possible heirs and each person's position. A property, bank account, employment payment and company interest may require different acts and different authority.

Only after those facts are mapped can the family decide whether it needs a power of attorney, more relationship evidence or a dispute strategy.

Build a complete heir and location table

Record the deceased person's details, spouse, children, parents, earlier marriages and any person who died earlier. For every possible heir, note the present country or city, whether they can be contacted, whether they agree to cooperate and which records they hold.

Also identify anyone who has collected rent, held original property papers, managed a bank card or received money. Control of an asset does not decide entitlement, but it can create an immediate evidence or accounting issue.

Group people by the action they can take

The first group is ready to cooperate and can prepare identity, signature and authority records. The second agrees in principle but needs a practical arrangement because of travel, health or location. The third actively disagrees, refuses to provide records or cannot be contacted.

Do not describe all three groups as “not signing”. Their situations require different responses.

Define authority task by task

“Handle the inheritance” may not tell an institution whether the representative can search a file, submit an application, sign a transfer, receive funds, negotiate a settlement or take part in proceedings. List the actual tasks and confirm which ones may be delegated.

Keep receipt of money and final allocation as separate decisions. A representative's ability to receive funds does not by itself decide how the family will divide them.

Preserve common facts even if agreement is incomplete

The family may not yet agree on distribution, but can still record the death, family tree, property address, bank clues and who holds the documents. Preserve messages, rent records and transfers without turning every conversation into an argument about shares.

Seek an early legal review where an asset may be disposed of, money is moving, an heir's identity is disputed, a will is challenged, or repeated requests for signature have produced no clear response.
