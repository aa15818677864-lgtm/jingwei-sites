Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

The registered nominee dies first: evidence for a Mainland property inheritance dispute

A home may be registered to one relative while another family member supplied the money. If the registered owner dies first, heirs may have to resolve both the estate and whether a nominee arrangement existed. Preserve the evidence; payment alone does not automatically prove title.

Published: 2026-10-23

Prove registration, payment and use separately

Collect the register, purchase contract, payment and mortgage records, tax receipts, renovation expenses and evidence of long-term use. Each proves a different fact: registration, funding or management are not interchangeable.

Look for contemporaneous evidence of the arrangement

Keep the original nominee agreement, messages, repayment or income records, handover material and property-management correspondence with their full context. A later statement or one person’s recollection may be insufficient. Never alter a transfer note or create a signature.

Assess litigation and preservation early

If heirs say the registered owner alone owned the home, or a sale, mortgage or enforcement is imminent, assess the proper claim and whether evidence or property preservation is needed. Obtain registry and case materials from the property’s Mainland location before selecting a procedure.

Documents and points to check

  • Title register, purchase contract and change history.
  • Original payment, mortgage and tax records.
  • The nominee agreement and complete electronic messages.
  • Handover, occupation, rental, management and repair records.
  • Other heirs’ claims and any litigation or disposal risk.
