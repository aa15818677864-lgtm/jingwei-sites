Start with the situation itself

For a first discussion, prepare the property location, registered owner, family relationships, documents already held and the point where the transfer is stuck. An address and a death certificate rarely tell the whole story. A useful first consultation should turn the family, property, records and immediate obstacles into one clear working picture.

Prepare five groups of information

Start with the deceased person's full name, date and place of death, last usual residence, and any Hong Kong or Mainland death record. Next, list the spouse, children and parents who were alive at the relevant time, together with any remarriage, earlier death or long period without contact.

Then record the home as specifically as possible: city, address, estate or village, registered owner, and whether it is mortgaged, let or affected by redevelopment. Group the title certificate, sale contract, old household records, marriage records, will and powers of attorney. Finally, state the present obstacle: a relative is withholding title papers, rent is being collected without an account, or the registry has asked for more documents without identifying the gap.

Do not wait until every document is complete

An initial review does not require a perfect file. Preparing a declaration, notarised document or power of attorney too early can create more work if the purpose, name format or authority is wrong. It is usually more efficient to identify the property, city and people first, then decide which document is actually needed.

Use a short factual summary

A clear opening summary can be simple: when and where the person died; who the possible heirs are and where they live; which Mainland property are known; which records are available; whether the family can cooperate; and which issue needs attention first.

If something is unknown, say so. An honest gap is more useful than an estimate presented as fact.

A useful first review should produce a next-task list

The first conversation should not promise a completion date without seeing the records. It should identify the next three checks, the documents that should not yet be prepared, the people who need to participate, and whether to begin with a title search, inheritance proof, transfer or property dispute.
