First Call About a Mainland Estate: Five Things a Hong Kong Family Should Prepare

When a relative dies, the first question is often whether a Mainland property can be transferred immediately.

Start with the situation itself

When a relative dies, the first question is often whether a Mainland property can be transferred immediately. That is understandable, but an address and a death certificate rarely tell the whole story. A useful first consultation should turn the family, assets, records and immediate obstacles into one clear working picture.

Prepare five groups of information

Start with the deceased person's full name, date and place of death, last usual residence, and any Hong Kong or Mainland death record. Next, list the spouse, children and parents who were alive at the relevant time, together with any remarriage, earlier death or long period without contact.

Then record the known assets as specifically as possible: city, property address, bank, company or former employer. Keep the existing records in separate groups, such as property papers, bank messages, old household records, marriage records, wills and powers of attorney. Finally, state the present obstacle: a relative is withholding records, rent is being collected without an account, or an institution has asked for more documents without identifying the gap.

Do not wait until every document is complete

An initial review does not require a perfect file. Preparing a declaration, notarised document or power of attorney too early can create more work if the purpose, name format or authority is wrong. It is usually more efficient to identify the asset, city and people first, then decide which document is actually needed.

Use a short factual summary

A clear opening summary can be simple: when and where the person died; who the possible heirs are and where they live; which Mainland assets are known; which records are available; whether the family can cooperate; and which issue needs attention first.

If something is unknown, say so. An honest gap is more useful than an estimate presented as fact.

A useful first review should produce a next-task list

The first conversation should not promise a completion date without seeing the records. It should identify the next three checks, the documents that should not yet be prepared, the people who need to participate, and whether the property, bank or dispute work should begin first.
