Start with the situation itself

Before inherited Mainland property can be transferred, map the possible heirs, where they live, whether they can be reached and whether they agree. Difficulties appear later: a child from an earlier marriage was omitted, a relative died before the deceased and the next generation was never listed, or someone has been out of contact for years and was simply left off the file. A working family tree exposes these gaps early.

Show each relationship in one view

Place the deceased person in the centre. Add parents above, every marriage or partner relationship beside them, and all children below. For each person, record whether they are alive, where they now live and whether they can be contacted. If someone died earlier, continue the branch to their children rather than ending it with a mark.

Adoption, stepchildren, children born outside marriage, name changes and differences between Hong Kong and Mainland records should be marked for verification. The chart is not a legal conclusion. It is a map of the facts that may change the analysis.

Record cooperation separately from identity

Being a possible heir and being ready to cooperate are different questions. Use a separate status for each person: confirmed and cooperative, contactable but abroad, position unclear, unwilling to participate, or currently uncontactable.

This also helps plan signatures. A person in Hong Kong, Mainland China or another country may need a different document route. Discovering an omitted person only after documents have been prepared can force the family to repeat the work.

Do not fill gaps with family assumptions

Terms such as “god-daughter”, “no longer part of the family” or “the one who looked after everything” do not by themselves settle inheritance status. Record the family's wording and the documents behind it, but do not remove or add a person merely to make the chart look complete.

A useful family tree should let an independent reader understand, within a few minutes, who died, who may need to be considered, where each person is, what remains uncertain and where cooperation may break down.
