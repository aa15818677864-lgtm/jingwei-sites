Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

A tenant leaves a Mainland estate property: reconciling the deposit, rent and handover

When a deceased owner's tenant wants to leave, a Hong Kong family needs more than a set of keys. The deposit may still carry a repayment obligation. Establish the tenancy position and the person authorised to act, then reconcile the money and condition of the property separately.

Published: 2026-10-23

Check the agreement and authority first

A change of ownership during the tenant's contractual occupation generally does not invalidate a Mainland tenancy. The landlord's death alone is not a reason to demand immediate departure or retain the entire deposit. Review the term, notice provisions, early termination arrangements and subsequent agreements.

A relative who holds the keys or previously collected rent is not automatically entitled to terminate the agreement, agree compensation or refund money for everyone. Confirm the estate administration or agency arrangement. Specify who can inspect, sign a handover record, agree deductions and make payment. If authority is disputed, preserve records and avoid giving the tenant contradictory payment instructions.

Keep the deposit separate from rental income

Use distinct entries for the deposit received, rent and prepaid rent, unpaid charges, proposed deductions and proposed refund. Money in the deceased's account is not necessarily available for immediate distribution. If a relative refunds the tenant from personal funds, retain the transfer and the tenant's acknowledgement to prevent duplicate repayment.

Each deduction needs a contractual and factual basis. Identify the unpaid period, meter reading or damaged item, with appropriate records and photographs. Ordinary wear should be distinguished from damage for which the tenant is responsible. A broad label such as refurbishment cost does not explain why an entire deposit should be retained. Show agreed and disputed items separately.

Document the physical and financial handover

The handover record should identify the property, date, people present, keys and access cards, fixtures, meter readings and unresolved work. Attach identifiable photographs. If inspection or quotations remain outstanding, say so rather than signing an unrestricted statement that everything has been settled.

Where the refund and return of possession happen at different times, record the remaining conditions, payment destination and person following up. File the signed handover, reconciliation and payment evidence in the estate accounts. Explain the transaction to the relevant family members. A completed checkout does not itself complete inheritance registration or settle how the estate is ultimately divided.

Checkout records to retain

  • Tenancy, amendments and termination notice.
  • Deposit receipt and rental payment history.
  • Evidence of administration or agency authority.
  • Itemised charges, readings and condition photographs.
  • Signed handover and unresolved issues.
  • Refund evidence and estate accounting entry.
