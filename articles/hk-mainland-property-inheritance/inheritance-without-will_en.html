No Will: Which Family Members Should a Hong Kong Family Identify First?

When there is no will, families often begin with the people who are presently in contact.

Last updated: 2026-07-23

This may be close to what your family is facing

  • Fix the family position at the date of death
  • Do not replace a formal decision with “they do not want it”
  • Separate the assets into workstreams

Start with the situation itself

When there is no will, families often begin with the people who are presently in contact. The safer starting point is the family position at the date of death. A spouse, child or parent who later died, moved abroad or stopped speaking to the family may still affect the sequence of work.

Fix the family position at the date of death

Record the deceased person's spouse, children and parents as at that date. Add earlier marriages, children from different relationships, adoption, a child who died earlier and any family member whose status is uncertain. If a person later died, keep that event as a separate point on the timeline rather than removing the person from the chart.

The aim is to identify who may need to be considered and what must still be verified. It is not to make a final allocation from family memory alone.

Do not replace a formal decision with “they do not want it”

A relative may say informally that they are not interested, may live abroad or may not reply to messages. That does not mean another family member should speak on their behalf. Record the person's current position, what information they have received and whether a document or decision is actually required.

This distinction is particularly important where one relative controls the property, rent, bank card or original records.

Separate the assets into workstreams

A family may be dealing with property, savings, employment-related payments and company interests at the same time. Identifying the family is a common first step, but each asset may have a different enquiry, transfer and document route.

List the people once, then open a separate workstream for each asset. This is more reliable than taking one document package to every institution.

Keep one complete people list

For each person, record the name, relationship, whether they were alive at the date of death, current location, contact status, present position and records held. Mark every uncertain point and its source.

No will does not automatically make a matter simple or complicated. The people and timeline must be clear before the family can sensibly discuss signatures, allocation or the handling of a particular asset.
