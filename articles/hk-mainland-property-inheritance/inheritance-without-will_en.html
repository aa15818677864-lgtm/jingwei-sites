Start with the situation itself

Without a will, do not assume the property can simply be divided equally. Map the family at the date of death and identify who may need to join the transfer. The safer starting point is the family position at the date of death. A spouse, child or parent who later died, moved abroad or stopped speaking to the family may still affect the sequence of work.

Fix the family position at the date of death

Record the deceased person's spouse, children and parents as at that date. Add earlier marriages, children from different relationships, adoption, a child who died earlier and any family member whose status is uncertain. If a person later died, keep that event as a separate point on the timeline rather than removing the person from the chart.

The aim is to identify who may need to be considered and what must still be verified. It is not to make a final allocation from family memory alone.

Do not replace a formal decision with “they do not want it”

A relative may say informally that they are not interested, may live abroad or may not reply to messages. That does not mean another family member should speak on their behalf. Record the person's current position, what information they have received and whether a document or decision is actually required.

This distinction is particularly important where one relative occupies the property, collects rent or controls the keys and original title records.

Separate each property by title and use

A family may be dealing with a commercial flat, ancestral or homestead housing, a self-built home, relocation housing, or a mortgaged or tenanted property. Identifying the family is a common first step, but each property can have a different search, evidence and transfer route.

List the people once, then open a separate workstream for each home. This is more reliable than taking one document package to every registration office.

Keep one complete people list

For each person, record the name, relationship, whether they were alive at the date of death, current location, contact status, present position and records held. Mark every uncertain point and its source.

No will does not automatically make a matter simple or complicated. The people and timeline must be clear before the family can sensibly discuss signatures, shares or the transfer of a particular property.
