Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

Mainland inheritance records differ from the old property certificate: a comparison sheet for Hong Kong families

A different address, area or owner entry on a Mainland registry search does not tell the whole story. Keep the old certificate and the new result intact. A field-by-field comparison helps identify whether the issue concerns historical identification, a possible registry error or ownership itself.

Published: 2026-10-23

Identify the property before explaining the difference

Compare the location, old certificate number, real estate unit identifier, owner names, co-ownership entry, area and acquisition date. Address renumbering, replacement certificates, revised surveys and later transactions can produce different records. Similar descriptions alone do not establish that two documents concern the same legal property.

Give each discrepancy its own row. Record the exact entry, issuer and date from both documents, then add the unanswered question. Keep complete scans, including plans and attachments. Family recollections can guide an archive search, but should not be presented as an official link between two addresses. Never edit an original document image to make the entries match.

Distinguish access to records from proof of inheritance

The Mainland Civil Code gives the real estate register a central evidential role. Where a certificate and the register differ, the register generally prevails unless evidence establishes that it is erroneous. Check what your search actually covers: a current summary may not explain previous changes.

Guangzhou's published guidance asks heirs seeking a deceased relative's records for identification, evidence of death and material establishing the inheritance relationship, among other application documents. This is a local example. Ask the registry where the property is situated about its own requirements. A Hong Kong family using a representative should also confirm the required authority and treatment of documents formed outside the Mainland. Receiving a search result does not finally establish every heir's entitlement.

Use the comparison to choose the next step

For an address link, locate the responsible archive or authority. For a suspected register error, identify the precise entry and evidence supporting correction. If another owner disputes the correction, or the problem is who owns the property, a separate ownership issue may need resolution.

Send the comparison sheet with the supporting documents and ask the handling institution to identify each missing item and its acceptable issuer. Keep confirmed facts separate from assumptions, particularly where a discrepancy concerns co-ownership shares.

Prepare for the first review

  • Complete old certificate and current search result.
  • A dated discrepancy sheet with exact entries.
  • Available purchase, survey and address-change records.
  • Death, relationship and current identity evidence.
  • The representative's authority and local requirements.
  • A log of unanswered questions and institutional replies.
