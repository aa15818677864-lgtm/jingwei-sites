Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

A Mainland public-housing tenant dies: check occupancy eligibility and local rules

The death of a named public-housing tenant does not automatically transfer ownership to heirs. First determine whether the home remains publicly owned, was purchased under a reform scheme, or is subject to another arrangement. Then review the lease, occupancy evidence and local succession rules for tenancy.

Published: 2026-10-24

Identify whether the document records ownership or tenancy

A title certificate and registry entry differ from a public-housing lease certificate. If the deceased held only a tenancy, kinship alone does not make the building divisible estate property.

Eligibility depends on location and housing type

Local rules may consider co-residence, household registration, housing conditions, lease terms and the owner’s policy. Direct-managed, employer-managed and subsidised housing can follow different routes. Do not apply one city’s order to another.

Contact the housing owner or authority in writing

Prepare the lease, rent records, co-resident evidence, death certificate and family positions. Ask the owner or housing authority about deadlines, eligibility review and dispute handling. If relatives disagree, preserve rent and use records and avoid unilateral lock changes or subletting.

Documents and points to check

  • Public-housing lease, tenancy certificate and owner details.
  • Evidence of co-residence and household registration.
  • Rent payments and correspondence with the housing manager.
  • Local tenant-change rules and application deadline.
  • Family discussions and unresolved disagreements.
