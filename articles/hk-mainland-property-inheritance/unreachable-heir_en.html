Start with the situation itself

The family can trace the property and organise documents, but that does not mean the missing heir's possible share can be ignored when completing the transfer. Preparing information while a person is missing is different from completing a transfer or distribution as if the person did not exist.

Check whether the person is truly uncontactable

Record the date of last contact, known telephone numbers and addresses, mutual relatives, employment history and migration clues. Keep a reasonable log of contact attempts, but do not publish unnecessary personal details or impersonate someone to obtain information.

Sometimes “missing” means the relationship is poor and nobody has made a structured attempt. In other cases, the contact details are known but the person refuses to respond. The distinction matters.

Property records can be organised before transfer

The family can usually organise records, identify the property, check who occupies it, record who collects rent and preserve title papers without pretending that ownership has already changed.

Transferring or selling the property, changing the tenancy, or signing a final ownership allocation is a different level of action. A temporary lack of contact should not be used to assume that a possible heir has no position.

Keep a search timeline

For each attempt, record the date, method, result and any new lead. Information from mutual contacts should identify its source. This timeline is not only evidence that a search was attempted; it helps a lawyer assess what reasonable steps remain.

Do not speak for the missing person

Statements such as “they once said they wanted nothing” or “they had no relationship with the deceased” should not be converted into a decision on that person's behalf. The family may not know what information or arrangement the person previously received.

Organise the person's identity, search record, current property position and any time-sensitive risk. Then separate the work that can continue from the transfer or disposal decisions that need a clearer route for dealing with the missing person.
