Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

A Mainland property is still undivided when an heir dies: map both successions first

An heir may die before a Mainland property has been transferred out of the original owner's name. Registration alone does not answer whether that heir had a share in the estate. Hong Kong families should establish the order of deaths, whether the first inheritance was renounced, and who may succeed to the later deceased heir's interest.

Published: 2026-11-17

Which person died first?

If a child died before the original owner, representation under the statutory succession rules may be relevant. If the original owner died first and an heir died before division without renouncing, the issue is transmission of that heir's inheritance. These are different routes and may involve different people.

Place both death records and the property documents on a single dated timeline. Preserve the issuing details of Hong Kong and Mainland records. Family recollection can help locate a document but should not replace it.

Check the first inheritance before mapping the second

Article 1152 of the Mainland Civil Code addresses an heir who dies after succession begins but before division without having renounced, subject to a different arrangement in a will. Review the original owner's will, any renunciation, division agreement and earlier notarisation or court papers. The unchanged property certificate does not by itself resolve the issue.

List the original owner's heirs separately from the later deceased heir's own potential successors. The latter group depends on that person's relationships, will and applicable rules. This article addresses the Mainland property process and does not determine how a separate Hong Kong estate should be distributed.

Give the Mainland institution a two-level document map

For each person, record identity, relationship, death date where relevant, available proof and missing items. Ask the registry or notary where the property is located how Hong Kong documents and any representative's authority should be presented. A guide issued for another city is informative but is not a universal filing list.

If relatives dispute the first share, the effect of a renunciation or membership of the second group, preserve the underlying documents and ask whether a Mainland court process is required. A person authorised to deliver papers should not sign a division decision for everyone without the relevant authority.

Before contacting the Mainland handling institution

  • Both death dates and supporting records.
  • Property title, acquisition and co-ownership records.
  • Identity and relationship evidence for both groups.
  • Relevant wills, renunciations and division papers.
  • Local requirements for Hong Kong documents and authority.
  • A list of disputed people, shares and documents.
