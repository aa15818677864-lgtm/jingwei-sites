Start with the situation itself

When names differ across Hong Kong identity records, older household records and the property registration, build a version table before deciding what needs to be proved. Saying “everyone knows this is the same person” is not enough. The records need a visible connection.

List every version without hiding the differences

Copy the name exactly as it appears on each identity or property record, including punctuation, order of romanised names and identity numbers. Add the document name, year and place of issue. Arrange early household, school or employment records, later Hong Kong identity records, and title certificates, sale contracts and old property addresses on one timeline.

Do not alter a scan to make the names match. Even an ordinary script or spelling difference should be recorded. A completely different name may require a record that contains two stable identity clues.

Compare information that tends to remain stable

Date of birth, spouse and children, former address, identity number, photograph and employer may help connect different names. Any one of these may also contain an error, so look for several points that form a consistent timeline.

The weakest approach is to use one recent identity document to explain every older property record without showing when and how the name changed.

Ask what the receiving institution cannot match

A registry may be unable to match the owner named on an old title, or the problem may be the link between the deceased and an heir. Those are different gaps and may require different supporting records.

Keep family recollection separate from objective records

Family members can provide useful background, but “that is what we always called him” is not a document connection. Record who supplied the recollection, then look for independent support. Where no record can presently be found, mark the gap instead of guessing.

Once the identity timeline is clear, the property search, inheritance confirmation and transfer can use the same foundation rather than rebuilding the explanation each time.
