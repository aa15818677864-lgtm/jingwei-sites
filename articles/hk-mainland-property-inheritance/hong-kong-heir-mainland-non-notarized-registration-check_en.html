Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

Must Hong Kong heirs notarise a Mainland property inheritance before registration?

Do not assume the same answer applies to every Mainland property. Some local registries provide a non-notarised inheritance route. Whether a Hong Kong heir can use it, and how Hong Kong documents must be presented, should be confirmed with the registry where the property is located.

Published: 2026-11-17

Ask the registry for this property

Fuzhou's registration centre publishes a document list for non-notarised inheritance applications. That is evidence of a local route, not a nationwide checklist. Ask the registry for the property's city whether this route is available, who must attend, whether representation is accepted and how documents made in Hong Kong should be submitted.

The underlying inheritance still needs evidence

Fuzhou's published list includes identity, death, family relationship, property-right and will materials. It also addresses deceased heirs, renunciation and marital property agreements. A route without an inheritance notarisation does not dispense with examination of these facts.

If the heirs dispute the will, their entitlement or ownership shares, list the contested facts and evidence before selecting a registration route. An administrative application cannot itself resolve every substantive dispute.

Check each Hong Kong document by purpose

Death, marriage, birth and identity records prove different matters. Confirm the acceptable form and any handling requirements with the Mainland receiving authority before arranging authentication or forwarding. Keep a document-by-document list of issuer, purpose, original holder, registry response and missing item.

Before contacting the Mainland registry

  • Property location and current ownership evidence
  • Death and relationship records
  • Potential heirs and where they live
  • Any will, renunciation or marital property agreement
  • Purpose and issuer of each Hong Kong document
  • The local registry's attendance and agency requirements
