Start with the situation itself

For remote property searches, inheritance confirmation, transfer or sale, list each task before defining the authority so the document does not need to be signed again. “Full authority to handle the property inheritance” may sound broad, yet still fail to explain whether the representative can search records, sign a transfer application or handle rent or sale proceeds.

List the tasks before drafting the authority

Start with the actual sequence of work: search the property register, obtain copies, submit an application, receive notices, take part in discussions, complete registration, handle rent or sale proceeds, or participate in a property dispute. Not every matter needs every task.

For each item, ask whether it can be delegated, whether the receiving institution uses particular wording, and whether the authority would permit a sale, renunciation or receipt of substantial property proceeds. Wider authority is not automatically better.

Separate the property search, transfer and dispute powers

A property search, an inheritance transfer, a later sale and participation in court proceedings involve different acts. Even if one document covers several stages, state them separately. Where several relatives give authority, use consistent names and powers unless there is a deliberate reason for a difference.

Agree how rent or sale proceeds will be handled

Decide who may receive rent or sale proceeds, whether the representative may collect them, and how each receipt will be reported to the other family members. Authority to receive property proceeds and the final agreement on ownership shares are separate questions.

Read the document backwards before sending it

Ask the person who will actually use the document, or the lawyer coordinating the matter, to read it from the final task backwards. What can be searched? What can be signed at the registry? What can be done about rent, sale or a request for more records?

A power of attorney should be as broad as the confirmed work requires, but no broader than the family understands. Specific tasks reduce both rejection risk and unintended authority.
