Handling a Mainland Estate From Hong Kong: How Specific Should the Power of Attorney Be?

Start with the situation itself

The frustrating problem in remote estate work is often not the absence of a power of attorney, but discovering after it reaches Mainland China that one necessary act is missing. “Full authority to handle the inheritance” may sound broad, yet still fail to explain whether the representative can search records, sign a particular application or receive money.

List the tasks before drafting the authority

Start with the actual sequence of work: search property or account records, obtain copies, submit an application, receive notices, take part in discussions, complete registration, receive funds, or participate in a dispute. Not every matter needs every task.

For each item, ask whether it can be delegated, whether the receiving institution uses particular wording, and whether the authority would permit a sale, renunciation or receipt of substantial funds. Wider authority is not automatically better.

Do not compress property, banking and proceedings into one phrase

A property search and transfer, a bank enquiry and participation in court proceedings involve different acts. Even if one document covers several workstreams, state them separately. Where several relatives give authority, use consistent names and powers unless there is a deliberate reason for a difference.

Agree the receipt-of-funds arrangement separately

Decide whose account may receive money, whether the representative may collect it, and how the receipt will be reported to the other family members. Authority to receive funds and the final agreement on how to divide them are separate questions.

Read the document backwards before sending it

Ask the person who will actually use the document, or the lawyer coordinating the matter, to read it from the final task backwards. What can be signed at the bank? What can be submitted at the registry? Who can receive a notice or respond to a request for more records?

A power of attorney should be as broad as the confirmed work requires, but no broader than the family understands. Specific tasks reduce both rejection risk and unintended authority.
