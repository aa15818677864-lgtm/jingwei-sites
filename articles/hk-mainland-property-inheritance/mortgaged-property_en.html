A Mainland Estate Property Still Has a Mortgage: How Should Repayment and Inheritance Be Sequenced?

When a family discovers that a Mainland property is still being financed, two unhelpful reactions are common.

Last updated: 2026-07-23

This may be close to what your family is facing

  • Identify the actual liability
  • Connect temporary payments to the property plan
  • Check insurance and related contractual arrangements

Start with the situation itself

When a family discovers that a Mainland property is still being financed, two unhelpful reactions are common. One person rushes to pay the entire balance, fearing that the debt will grow without limit. Another assumes nobody needs to act until the title is transferred. A better approach is to check the loan, property and family decision separately before joining them into one plan.

Identify the actual liability

Locate the lender, loan agreement or repayment account, present balance, arrears, security over the property and the person currently making payments. Do not rely on a single message or confuse mortgage payments with management charges and other property expenses.

If detailed information requires an identity and authority review, organise the account, property and family records for a formal enquiry. Anyone who has made payments should keep every transfer and its stated purpose.

Connect temporary payments to the property plan

Whether the property will be retained, sold or taken by one family member affects future payments. One person should not continue paying indefinitely on the oral assumption that every amount will later be deducted from sale proceeds.

Use a temporary written arrangement: which payments are necessary to avoid immediate risk, who will advance them, how often the account will be shared, and whether those payments do or do not settle any ownership issue.

Check insurance and related contractual arrangements

Some loans may have insurance or another repayment arrangement, but the family should not rely on recollection. Locate the contract and debit records, ask the relevant institution, and then decide whether payments should continue or another process applies.

Calculate net value before discussing a sale

The market value is not the amount available for distribution. Put the remaining loan, arrears, necessary costs and supported family advances in the same calculation.

A mortgage does not automatically prevent inheritance, and it does not mean relatives should immediately pay the debt in full. Obtain reliable figures, preserve necessary payment records and make the repayment decision alongside the property plan.
