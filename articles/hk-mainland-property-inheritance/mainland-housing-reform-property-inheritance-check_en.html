Mainland property inheritance for Hong Kong families

Inheriting a Mainland housing-reform home: check title, payments and marital ownership first

A housing-reform home may have an old certificate, a purchase agreement and employer records that do not say the same thing. Before a Hong Kong family applies, establish what title the deceased actually acquired, whether any payment or land-related condition remains, and which rights form part of the estate.

Published: 2026-10-23

Identify the actual title

Housing-reform homes arose under different purchase arrangements. Read the register, sale agreement, payment records and any title restriction together. An old workplace allocation or a child’s contribution does not by itself establish sole ownership.

Separate a spouse’s share before calculating the estate

If the home was acquired during marriage, check the acquisition date, source of funds and any marital property agreement. A spouse’s own share must be separated before the deceased’s interest is treated as estate property.

Verify with the former employer and registry

Create a timeline from the reform-sale contract, receipts, adjustment payments, old certificate and current register. Ask the former employer whether its file survives, then confirm the local registry’s list. Requirements can vary by city and reform scheme.

Documents and points to check

  • The reform-sale contract and purchase-price category.
  • Payment, adjustment, maintenance-fund or land-income records.
  • Old certificate, current register and co-ownership entries.
  • Marriage records and any marital property agreement.
  • The employer archive contact and local registry’s response.
