Start with the situation itself

When an old property address, identity number or name format has changed, preserve each version and place it on a timeline with the ownership records. A street number may have changed, an administrative district may have been renamed, an old household registration may have been cancelled, and a Hong Kong identity record may use a different number. These differences do not necessarily make the records wrong, but they need a transparent comparison.

Preserve the original wording

Scan and transcribe each old record as it appears. Use a separate column for the current address or name, and identify the source of that comparison. Do not cover an old address on an image with a modern one; the original wording may later be needed to locate the historical file.

Handle identity numbers in the same way. Record the period, record type and place of issue for each number, and look for material that connects the old and new versions. Keep partial numbers as clues, but avoid distributing complete sensitive numbers unnecessarily.

Put every change on a timeline

Start with early residence or household records, then add migration to Hong Kong, identity changes, the property purchase or account opening, and the death. Differences that appear contradictory in isolation often become understandable when placed in time order.

Link an old address to the property history

An older home may be described only by a village, work unit or former street number. Utility bills, management charges, tenancy records, purchase receipts, relocation notices and dated photographs can help locate it. They may not decide ownership by themselves, but they can identify the place and its history.

Provide a one-page differences schedule

Do not expect a case officer to work out the connections across many records. Prepare a table showing the original entry, present entry, approximate date of change and supporting material. Mark what remains uncertain.

The value of an old record lies in preserving history, not in making every page look modern. Keep the original intact and build a clear bridge to the current information.
