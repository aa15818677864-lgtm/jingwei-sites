Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

Before listing a Mainland wealth product as estate cash, check its manager and redemption terms

A wealth product shown in a bank account is not necessarily a cash deposit. It may be locked, subject to a holding period or registered with a separate manager. Identify the product, units, valuation date and redemption rules before seeking redemption or a non-trade transfer.

Published: 2026-10-23

Check with both the bank and product manager

Obtain the product terms, risk disclosure, holding statement, latest valuation and transaction status. The bank may only distribute the product; redemption or transfer may be handled by the manager. Confirm both parties’ requirements first.

Distinguish redemption from inheritance transfer

A regulatory notice says banks should guide applicants to seek redemption or non-trade transfer from the product manager. The contract, dealing window and investor-suitability rules still matter. Simplified small-deposit procedures do not mean every investment can be withdrawn directly.

Update the estate inventory from the settlement statement

Record the application date, unit value, redemption amount, fees, receiving account and unsettled returns. If heirs have not agreed on shares, list the product and valuation date without treating its original purchase price as its current value.

Documents and points to check

  • Product name, manager and sales channel.
  • Units, valuation date and any lock or freeze.
  • Redemption, non-trade transfer and investor eligibility rules.
  • Succession, death and claimant identity documents.
  • Settlement amount, fees and payment evidence.
