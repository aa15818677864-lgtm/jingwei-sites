A Mainland patent applicant has died: what should a Hong Kong family check first?

If a Hong Kong family knows that a relative filed a patent application in Mainland China but cannot find the latest notice, do not treat “there was a patent” as proof of a transferable right. Key point first

If a Hong Kong family knows that a relative filed a patent application in Mainland China but cannot find the latest notice, do not treat “there was a patent” as proof of a transferable right. First distinguish an application still pending, a granted patent, an assignment, and an expired or terminated right. The status affects who may receive notices, provide documents or deal with licensing revenue.

First question to settle

Preserve the application number, inventor and applicant names, agent correspondence and payment records. An inventor is not necessarily the applicant; an employee invention or a company-owned right may not be personal estate. Confirm the receiving office’s document route before using Hong Kong material for a Mainland change. See company equity inheritance and institution-specific files.
