One Mainland Estate, Three Recipients: Build Separate Packs for Property, Bank and Company

The family can share one identity and relationship record. It should not send the same bundle unchanged to the property registry, bank and company. Each asset line has a different recipient, purpose and evidential gap.

Last updated：2026-08-04

Three points to keep in view

  • Share a fact record, not a universal pack
  • Separate property, bank and company lines
  • Log every version, original and response

The short answer: one fact record, three recipient-specific packs

Start with a common record of the deceased's identity, date of death, name variations, possible heirs and contact status. Then create separate property, bank-product and company-share packs. Each pack should name the recipient, the requested action, the facts that must be established and the missing records.

Consistency is not the same as acceptance. A common record helps the family give the same facts, but each recipient must still confirm what form of original, certified copy, translation or authority it requires.

A composite scenario: one scanned bundle goes to three places

A father dies in Hong Kong, leaving a Shenzhen apartment, a bank relationship containing deposits and investment products, and shares in a private company. His daughter scans the death, identity, relationship and will documents into one file and sends it to the property counter, a bank relationship manager and the company's finance contact.

The recipients ask different questions. The property line needs ownership and participant details. The bank first separates deposits from other products. The company asks for its shareholder records, articles and the person authorised to act for it. The problem is not a shortage of paper. The pack was never organised around the asset and recipient.

Create a common fact record, not a universal application

Record every name used by the deceased, identity-number matches, date of death, marital and family relationships, possible heirs, will clues and each person's contact status. Link each statement to its source and mark what remains unverified.

This prevents relatives from giving conflicting accounts. It does not replace evidence of ownership, product records, company documents or a recipient's own forms.

The property pack must identify the right, applicant and transfer route

Keep the address, ownership record, registered name, co-ownership, mortgage or restriction clues, and any property-specific will or distribution material together. A photograph of a title record does not by itself settle the extent of the property or the deceased's share.

If a person cannot attend, intends to renounce, has a name mismatch or sits behind a missing link in the family chain, ask the actual registration office how that gap is to be addressed. Title checks and requirement confirmation may start early; signing and transfer must follow the route the recipient confirms.

Split the bank pack by product before discussing a balance

Use a separate page for each institution and account, then distinguish current and fixed deposits, wealth products, funds, insurance, safe custody and debts. Where there is only a clue, record its source, account suffix and last known use instead of presenting an estimated balance as fact.

Different products may be handled by different teams and may require different proof. Ask what is needed to identify, freeze, release or transfer each product before sending the only original document.

Separate company shares from property owned by the company

Obtain the registration record, register of members, articles, contribution history, transfer records and dividend information. First establish what interest the deceased held. A home, account or vehicle owned by the company is not automatically an individual estate asset that heirs can divide directly.

Identify who can receive notice for the company, who may update its shareholder record and whether its articles or existing arrangements affect succession. A family-relationship bundle alone cannot answer questions about percentage ownership, governance or liabilities.

Use a purpose matrix: what fact must this document prove?

Give each document a row and record the recipient, purpose, required version, original or copy status, translation or verification step, submission date and response. The same death record may appear in all three packs, but its supporting material and purpose may differ.

When a recipient asks vaguely for notarisation or relationship proof, clarify whether the gap concerns death, identity consistency, the complete family chain, signature authenticity or authority. A precise question can prevent an unnecessary remake of the entire bundle.

Keep one original-and-version ledger

Assign one custodian to each original. Date every scan and log the recipient, purpose, page count, delivery and return of each dispatch. Relatives should not revise relationship charts or powers independently and then assume they are using the same version.

Pause if requirements conflict, an original cannot be located, the company withholds basic records or someone asks for a blank signature. Preserve the question and response before deciding whether to obtain more evidence, change route or address a dispute.

Minimum fields for the three packs

  • Common relationship map, name table and timeline
  • Recipient and purpose for each asset
  • Property ownership, share and burden clues
  • Bank, account and product classification
  • Company registration, articles, members and contribution records
  • Fact each document is meant to establish
  • Original, certified-copy, translation and authority plan
  • Version submitted, recipient response and next gap
