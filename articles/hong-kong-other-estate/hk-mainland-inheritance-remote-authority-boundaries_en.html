Authorising a Mainland Inheritance Matter from Hong Kong: Where Should the Scope End?

A Hong Kong resident may authorise someone to handle steps in a Mainland China inheritance matter. A general instruction to “handle the inheritance,” however, can blur the line between administrative assistance and decisions that change legal rights. The authority should identify each permitted task and address separately any power to sign a distribution agreement, renounce an inheritance, receive funds, or dispose of property.

Published: 2026-10-23

Separate administrative steps from dispositive acts

Start with a list of tasks required in Mainland China: asking a bank, registry, or notary office about documents; filing or supplementing papers; collecting a document; and communicating with the relevant institution. Tie each task to a known asset, institution, and purpose. A phrase such as “full authority over the deceased’s property” may not tell the institution or other heirs whether the agent can submit papers only or can make decisions about the estate.

Signing a distribution agreement, renouncing an inheritance, receiving money, or transferring or selling a home may directly affect rights. These acts should not be bundled into a general authority for routine administration. If the client intends only to authorise document handling, the wording should not imply that the agent may also decide shares or dispose of assets.

Match the authority to the receiving Mainland procedure

Identify the asset type and location, then ask the institution handling that step what form of authority it accepts. Confirm whether it requires a prescribed format, notarisation or transfer formalities for a document signed in Hong Kong, and an original rather than a copy. An identity, family relationship, or authority document created in Hong Kong may need specific formalities before use in a Mainland procedure. A general power of attorney should not be assumed to work at every bank, registry, or notary office.

If the heirs are not yet identified, the status of a will is unclear, or relatives disagree about distribution, consider beginning with authority for inquiries and document collection. Further authority can be considered once the next step is clear. This keeps the authority aligned with the question the family is actually trying to resolve.

Keep written instructions and a record of expenses

Before signing, make a written task list showing what the agent may do, what may be signed, and which decisions require the client’s prior instruction. If money may be advanced, received, or spent, specify how supporting records and reports will be provided. A power of attorney, an engagement agreement, and an agreement among heirs serve different purposes; one should not be treated as a substitute for another.

Before delivering the authority

  • Confirm the Mainland asset, its location, and receiving institution.
  • Separate inquiries, filings, signatures, and disposal powers.
  • State whether renunciation, distribution, sale, or receipt of funds is excluded or separately authorised.
  • Check execution, notarisation, transfer, and original-document requirements.
  • Keep instructions, document versions, expense records, and progress reports.
