Can a Hong Kong Family Search a Deceased Relative’s Mainland Bank Records and Withdraw the Money?

Searching for account information and applying to withdraw an inheritance are separate steps. An eligible first-order heir may request certain transaction records, but a balance does not establish who may receive the funds or settle the estate’s distribution.

Published: 2026-10-23

Identify the information you need

Families in Hong Kong may know that a relative used a Mainland bank without knowing the account number or branch. Gather the deceased person’s name, identity details, former addresses, bank cards, passbooks, and messages that point to an institution. Also prepare proof of death and records showing the applicant’s relationship. Ask the relevant bank which channel it accepts and what documents it requires. Do not assume one bank can search every institution or financial product.

A 2024 joint notice allows a first-order heir, for a legitimate and proper reason, to request account transaction details for the period after death and the six months before death. This is a defined period, not general authority to review every financial record throughout the deceased person’s life. The bank will assess identity, relationship, and reason under its procedure.

A search result is not withdrawal authority

A bank response can identify an account, confirm a balance, or clarify relevant transactions. It is useful evidence, but does not replace proof of inheritance rights and does not resolve disagreement about a will, shares, or authority for one person to collect for others. Record the account, dates, balance, and relevant transactions before choosing a withdrawal route.

Simplified small-deposit procedures have separate eligibility and aggregation rules. Ask whether the institution combines balances, which products count, and what documents the applicant must provide. If the balance is outside the simplified route, a will is disputed, or heirs disagree, the search may still help, but withdrawal may require separate succession evidence or a court document.

Prepare Hong Kong records for the bank

Prepare the applicant’s identity document, proof of death, family records, and account clues. If the bank requires a Hong Kong record to prove a relationship or authority, first ask which document it accepts. A Hong Kong notarial instrument for use in Mainland China is generally issued by a China-Appointed Attesting Officer and reviewed and transmitted by China Legal Services (Hong Kong) Limited. Confirm the required form and treatment of originals with the receiving bank.

Keep a log of each bank contacted, the date, question, and response. If materials are incomplete, ask for a specific list of missing items and an accepted submission method. If the bank provides only a balance, explain why transaction details are needed, such as checking movements around the date of death or confirming the estate inventory. Ask whether the request can be made under the notice’s transaction-record procedure. Protect the information once received and share copies only with people who need them for the succession process. If family members disagree about a transfer, preserve the original bank record and clarify the issue before making assumptions about ownership.

Before requesting account information

  • Identity details, death proof, and relationship records
  • Bank cards, passbooks, messages, or transfer clues
  • The reason and period for the requested search
  • The bank’s requirements for Hong Kong records and representation
