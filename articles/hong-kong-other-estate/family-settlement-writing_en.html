The Family Has Agreed on the Estate: What Must the Written Settlement Cover?

“The eldest takes the property and everyone shares the cash” is a direction, not yet a plan that can complete registration, payment and accounting.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Confirm every participant
  • Identify each asset precisely
  • Connect payments, documents and deadlines

Translate the agreed shares into actions that can be completed

A written plan should answer who participates, which assets are covered, who receives each one, the value used, payment timing, document responsibilities, costs and what happens if a step cannot be completed.

A statement that assets are shared equally or one person will handle everything commonly leaves valuation, signatures, receipts and expenses unresolved.

Confirm every person who must participate

List the deceased, possible heirs, recipients under a will, executor or estate manager, and anyone who is only a contact or agent. Record each person's identity, location and representative-document needs.

A minor, a person unable to express a decision, an unreachable person or an unconfirmed family member cannot be resolved by adding that a relative will sign for them.

Identify each asset precisely

Use the property address and registration details, secure account schedules with institution and final digits, vehicle identifiers, and investment institution, product and units. Avoid the phrase all Mainland assets.

Mark unknown items as pending and state how newly found assets or debts will be reviewed rather than pretending the schedule is complete.

State who receives the asset and how value is balanced

If one person takes a property or vehicle, state the valuation date and method, balancing payment, account, instalments and consequences of delay. For continued co-ownership, address shares, use, income and major decisions.

For cash or investments, identify the balance date, verified deductions and treatment of later income or market movement.

Connect documents, costs and sequence

For each step, assign responsibility for death, relationship, will, authority and asset records, appointments, attendance and originals. Identify which receiving institution will confirm any cross-border document requirement.

Address taxes, registration, valuation, preservation, translation, travel and verified advances. If a cost is unknown, set a calculation and review method.

Plan for a failed step

State the notice and cure period if a transfer cannot complete, a payment is missed, documents are withheld or a new debt appears. Decide whether other distributions pause and how the family returns to negotiation or third-party help.

Check every page, schedule, date and signature method. For remote signing, keep one controlled version rather than circulating detached signature pages.
