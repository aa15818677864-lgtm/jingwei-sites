Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

A minor inherits Mainland property: what the guardian should know

A minor can inherit Mainland property, with a legal guardian generally applying on the child’s behalf. Hong Kong families should also prepare proof of guardianship, address any conflict of interest and understand the child-benefit review that may apply to a later sale or disposition.

Published: 2026-10-24

Prepare evidence of guardianship

A birth record, household record, adoption document or court guardianship order may establish the relationship. Ask the local registry what it accepts. If both guardian and child are in Hong Kong, also confirm identity documents and authority requirements.

Treat inheritance registration and later disposal as separate steps

Inheritance registration records the minor’s lawful share. A later sale, mortgage or waiver must protect the child’s interests. Do not ask the child to renounce or transfer property without payment merely to simplify the adults’ arrangement.

Address conflicts before the guardian signs

If the guardian is also another heir, purchaser or a person whose interests conflict with the child’s, disclose this and ask whether another guardian or independent representative is required. Keep valuations, reasons and payment records to show the decision protects the minor.

Documents and points to check

  • The minor’s identity, birth, adoption or household records.
  • Guardianship evidence and the guardian’s identification.
  • Death, family and title documents for registration.
  • Any conflict between the guardian and other heirs.
  • For a later disposal, records showing the child’s interests are protected.
