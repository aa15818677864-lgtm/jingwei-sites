Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

A dispute over a Mainland estate administrator: how an interested person can seek appointment

If heirs distrust one another, no one manages the assets or the executor’s role is disputed, first establish whether an administrator has already been determined by law. An interested person may apply to a Mainland court where the appointment itself is disputed; a separate application is not required in every inheritance case.

Published: 2026-10-24

Identify the existing administrator under the Civil Code

Check whether a will appoints an executor, heirs have chosen a person, or another statutory role applies. If identity and authority are undisputed, confirm the management scope. An administrator cannot rewrite a will or distribute property at will.

Prepare an application if the appointment is disputed

The Supreme People’s Court explains that an interested person may ask the court to appoint an administrator where the determination is disputed, using a special procedure. A Hong Kong applicant should organise identity, interest, the deceased’s residence, heirs, asset clues and the reason for disagreement.

Explain the need and suitability of a proposed person

Describe the tasks—preservation, inquiries, rent, debts or administration—and the existing conflict or risk. If proposing a person, provide their relationship, ability and potential conflicts. Once appointed, the administrator must keep accounts, report actions and protect estate interests.

Documents and points to check

  • Executor, any heir-selected administrator and current arrangements.
  • Applicant identity and legal interest in the estate.
  • Other heirs, residence clues and facts in dispute.
  • Assets needing protection or administration and urgency.
  • A proposed candidate’s ability, relationship and conflicts.
