Other Mainland Estate Assets / Successive deaths

Grandfather died, then father died before the estate was divided: build two succession layers

An unchanged property register does not mean nothing passed between the two deaths. Each death can create another layer of people and interests that must be identified.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Build a separate snapshot for each death
  • An undivided interest may enter the second estate
  • Do not merge two heir groups into one vague agreement

Why one flat needs two family diagrams

This composite example reflects recurring enquiries. Mr Wong died in 2018 leaving a Zhongshan flat. His three children never completed a division and the register remained in his name. The eldest son died in Hong Kong in 2024, leaving a wife and two children. In 2026 the family plans to sell the flat.

The surviving siblings say the eldest son's family has no place because title never changed. His family says he acquired an interest when his father died. The issue is whether each death created a distinct layer of succession, not whose name still appears on the certificate.

The short answer: calculate the father's place in the grandfather's estate, then place that interest in the father's estate

Layer one is fixed at the grandfather's death: identify his estate and the people then entitled to participate. If the father was alive, he may have acquired an undivided estate interest. Layer two is fixed at the father's death: determine who takes that interest together with his other property.

Continued registration in the grandfather's name only shows that registration was unfinished. It does not prove that no intermediate interest arose. Equally, the father's family cannot skip layer one and claim a fixed percentage of the entire flat without testing both layers, wills and pre-existing ownership.

Layer one: freeze the people and assets at the grandfather's death

List the grandfather's spouse, children, parents, any will and the property's ownership at that date. Separate any co-owner or surviving spouse interest first. Whether the father was alive on that date is central.

Record post-death rent, mortgage, repairs, occupation and agreements. Later management may not alter the opening share but it affects accounts and evidence.

Layer two: include the father's undivided interest in his own estate

When the father died, his inventory should include not only assets registered in Hong Kong or the Mainland but his unresolved interest in the grandfather's estate. The scope of that interest is calculated through layer one.

Create a second family diagram for the father's spouse, children, parents and any will. His wife may not inherit directly from the grandfather, yet may take through the father's intervening interest.

Keep two document chains even though the flat is the same

Separate the grandfather's death and relationship documents from the father's. Number the wills, waivers, agreements and identity discrepancies within each layer. A single mixed bundle makes it easy to omit someone alive at one of the dates.

A master table may show each estate, participants, disputed facts and possible outcomes, but it does not replace source documents. It is navigation, not evidence.

Account for years of rent, costs and signatories before sale

An undivided flat may have produced years of rent, repairs, management costs and tax, or been occupied by one relative. Record income and expenditure by year before calculating sale proceeds.

Signatories are not limited to the grandfather's children who remain alive today. If the father's branch passed into layer two, identify its participants, authorities and the traceable account that will receive proceeds.

A reliable agreement should show the sequence

Record the layer-one result, then how the father's interest passes at layer two, then who sells and how proceeds and costs are handled. A list of final percentages without the derivation hides mistakes.

If either layer contains a missing person, minor, identity dispute or contested will, mark the blockage. Searches, preservation and necessary management can proceed without waiting for every dispute to be resolved at once.

Two-layer working file

  • Assets and participants at the grandfather's death
  • Assets and participants at the father's death
  • Father's unresolved layer-one interest
  • Wills, spouse property and waivers at both dates
  • Rent, costs and management over the intervening years
