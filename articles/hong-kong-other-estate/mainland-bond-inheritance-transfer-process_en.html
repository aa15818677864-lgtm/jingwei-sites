Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

Mainland bond inheritance: locate the custodian and confirm a non-trade transfer

A deceased person may have held savings bonds, bank-counter bonds or other custody products. A Hong Kong family should identify the bank or registry holding the position, then ask whether inheritance can be processed as a non-trade transfer or requires maturity or redemption under the product rules.

Published: 2026-10-24

Identify the bond and custody channel

Look for bank statements, trade confirmations, custody cards, electronic statements and investor codes. Record the product, custodian, account number and payment dates. A bank-distributed bond is not a bank deposit and does not automatically follow deposit-withdrawal rules.

Ask whether inheritance uses a non-trade transfer

Some counter-bond rules allow a non-trade transfer after inheritance, generally requiring valid succession documents and a receiving account. Documents, counters and trading restrictions vary by bond and custodian. Obtain a written list before selling or sharing account credentials.

Reconcile interest, maturity and restrictions

Ask the custodian to confirm quantity, accrued interest, pledges or freezes, maturity and the receiving-account requirements. If heirs are split between Hong Kong and the Mainland, decide who applies, opens the account and grants authority. Keep the settlement record in the estate accounts.

Documents and points to check

  • Bond name, account number and custodian clues.
  • Trade confirmations, statements and bank records.
  • Succession, death and identity documents.
  • Requirements for the recipient custody account.
  • Interest, maturity, freeze and fee reconciliation.
