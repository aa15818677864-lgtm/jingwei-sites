Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

An estate inventory for Mainland assets: separate ownership, debts and evidence

In a cross-border inheritance, asset clues may be scattered across banks, registries, employers and platforms. Build an inventory that separates the deceased’s property, marital or family co-ownership, debts and unresolved interests before deciding what to investigate or report.

Published: 2026-10-23

Record clues without deciding ownership too early

For each item, note its type, institution, account or address, registered holder, valuation date, source document, co-owners and status. Mark it confirmed, pending or disputed; do not treat a family estimate as the final estate value.

Separate co-owned property before calculating the estate

Homes, deposits, securities and allowances may include a spouse’s or another person’s share. Check title and funding, separate proven co-ownership first, and retain a disputed category for unresolved property or platform balances.

Query institutions in batches and preserve results

Ask the local property registry, employer or annuity manager, bank, securities firm or platform through formal channels. For a court-assisted inquiry, prepare the target holder, specific lead and reason. Log dates, replies, missing items and new findings.

Documents and points to check

  • Asset type, institution and account or address clues.
  • Registered and co-owners with source documents.
  • Valuation date, debts, income and restrictions.
  • Inquiry destination, application date and written response.
  • Separate confirmed, pending and disputed entries.
