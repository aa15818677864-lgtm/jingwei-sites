Mainland Shares, Funds and Wealth Products: What Should a Hong Kong Family Trace First?

A bank statement shows only part of the picture. Listed shares, exchange-traded funds, open-ended funds and bank wealth products may each have a different account and service channel.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Classify products before estimating value
  • Identify the registrar and custodian
  • Preserve holdings around the date of death

Start with a product map

List every investment app, message, statement, dividend and redemption record. First classify the clue as a securities account, fund account, bank wealth product or another distributed product. The inquiry and transfer route can differ for each.

At this stage, match the institution, product, final account digits and date of the clue. Do not use one bank statement to estimate the entire portfolio.

Separate the products into four groups

Group exchange-listed securities, open-ended funds, bank-issued wealth products and unverified investment clues. A brand name alone is not enough; look for the full institution, product code, account digits, units and latest statement.

This classification prevents a family from treating a fund account as a deposit or assuming that every app balance is held by the same institution.

Identify the registrar, custodian and sales channel

A broker may provide the trading interface, a market infrastructure may record the holding, and a bank or platform may have sold the product. Asking the wrong channel can produce a correct statement that there is no deposit while leaving the investment untouched.

Record where it was bought, who issued it, who holds the record and who can provide a position statement. Ask about inquiry documents before transfer or redemption documents.

Use verifiable valuation dates

Market values change. Preserve positions and price evidence around the date of death, the first inquiry and the actual transfer. Label each source and never backfill today's price as the historical value.

Record later dividends, interest, rights, maturity payments and automatic redemptions separately, including the receiving account.

Do not trade through the deceased person's account

Even if the family knows the password, do not sell, redeem or change payment details as the deceased. Preserve complete statements and notices, then approach the institution through a person with an appropriate basis to inquire.

Market movement may create urgency, but an unauthorised trade can make the reason, proceeds and family accounting much harder to establish.

Create a portfolio checklist the family can share

Use columns for institution, product, code, account digits, units, valuation date, income destination and missing evidence. Mark each item only as a clue, confirmed or excluded.

Once the map is complete, the family can distinguish assets requiring an inquiry, a non-trading transfer, a maturity payment or further verification.
