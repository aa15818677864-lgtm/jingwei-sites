Evidence in a Mainland inheritance case may disappear: when to seek preservation

Surveillance footage, phone records, original receipts or account data in an estate dispute may be deleted, overwritten or become difficult to obtain. Identify where the evidence is, what it proves and why it may disappear, then promptly ask the court about preservation or judicial collection.

Published: 2026-10-23

Identify the evidence and its custodian

Avoid a general request to preserve all estate material. Specify the device, file, account, date range and person controlling it, and explain its link to a will, asset transfer, occupation or nominee dispute. Preserve lawful copies and record their source and date.

Explain urgency and choose the correct procedure

Under the Supreme People’s Court evidence rules, a trial-stage preservation request should be made before the evidence period expires. In urgent cases, pre-action preservation may be available where legal conditions are met. The court decides whether security or a particular measure is required.

Keep the application, court response and backup copies

State the evidence, location, fact to be proved, risk of loss and why it cannot be collected independently. Preserve lawful screenshots, original devices, delivery records and chain-of-custody notes. A mistaken request may cause loss, so tailor its scope to the dispute.

Documents and points to check

  • Evidence description, format, location and custodian.
  • The inheritance issue the evidence would prove.
  • Specific risk of loss or difficulty obtaining it later.
  • Evidence deadline, urgency and court details.
  • Existing copies, source notes and custody records.
