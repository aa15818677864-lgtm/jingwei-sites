A Relative Died with Money in a Mainland Medical Insurance Account: What Should the Hong Kong Family Check?

Do not spend the displayed balance or divide it from a screenshot. First stop all use of the deceased person's credentials and ask the participating city to terminate the record and verify the personal-account balance.

Last updated：2026-08-04

Three points to keep in view

  • Preserve the screen and transaction history
  • Separate the personal account from claims and family benefits
  • Use the administering body's verified figure

Short answer: verify the account and settlement figure before anyone claims the money

The family should stop all treatment, pharmacy, family-link and transfer activity under the deceased person's identity. Preserve the current screen, transaction history, participation clues and date of death, then contact the body administering the last known participation record.

Only the balance confirmed after death termination should move into the claimant and payment stage. An unsettled reimbursement, pooled benefit, family-sharing limit or separate survivor payment can follow a different process and should not be added to the personal-account balance.

A composite case: the phone shows a balance and a relative wants to spend it

A father dies in Hong Kong after retiring from work in Shenzhen. A cousin keeps his Mainland phone and sees more than RMB 20,000 on a medical insurance page. He suggests buying medicine for the widow before the account is closed. The daughter instead proposes using the screenshot as the estate figure.

Both proposals miss the same checks: what the screen represents, whether death settlement is complete, whether any post-death adjustment is pending, who may apply and where payment may be made. The safe order is to stop use, preserve evidence and obtain the participating city's itemised answer.

Identify the participating city and the type of balance

Use the old card, retirement papers, app labels, account entries, former employer and residence history to find the last participating city and administering body. A person who worked in several cities may have transferred, merged or unresolved records, so the final residence alone is not enough.

A page headed 'medical insurance balance' may show a personal account, consumption history, family sharing or pending items. Record the page name, update date, amount and transaction period; do not total every number and call it estate money.

Stop use immediately, but do not erase or empty the record

Tell anyone holding the phone, card or password not to obtain treatment, buy medicine, change family links or spend the balance. Keep full screenshots, messages, transaction details, card information and the date of the last authorised use.

If activity occurred after death, log the user, date, provider, amount, purpose and remaining funds. Do not recreate authority or alter the explanation. A complete account helps the administrator distinguish a valid settlement entry from mistaken use or an amount requiring repayment.

Ask for a death-termination and balance calculation

Ask who must report the death, when participation ends, through which month the personal account is calculated, how post-death credits are adjusted, the final amount open to application and what completion record will be issued. A figure given by telephone is a clue, not the family ledger.

The app or bank figure may change. A pre-death adjustment posted late, a post-death credit to be reversed, or a transaction not yet posted can all affect the calculation. The family should not promise distributions against the screen balance.

Keep the balance, pending claims and family benefits in separate columns

The remaining personal-account money follows the local death-settlement route. A reimbursement for treatment received before death may have its own deadline and supporting invoices. A payment available to family members can have a different eligible applicant.

Once separated, each column has its own claimant, records, deadline and payment route. A shared medical-insurance label does not make them one claim or subject them automatically to one inheritance calculation.

With several heirs, confirm the applicant and receiving account

Local procedures can differ for a spouse, child or parent, another heir, a beneficiary, joint applicants, an authorised representative and a simplified small-balance case. List every possible claimant before treating the cardholder as the family's representative.

Ask whether payment goes to the deceased person's existing account, a nominated Mainland account or the applicant's account, and whether a Hong Kong account can receive it. Receiving the money for convenience does not by itself give that person the whole balance.

Prepare a minimum Hong Kong document set, then follow the named checklist

Start with the death record, identity records, family-relationship clues, Mainland participation and banking information, a claimant list and any authority arrangement. For documents made in Hong Kong, ask the actual recipient about format, verification, validity and originals.

Do not authenticate, translate or dispatch every document at the outset. Until the city, claimant order and payment route are known, extensive document work can serve the wrong purpose. Obtain a named checklist first and then process each item.

Medical personal-account death-settlement checklist

  • Last participating city, administrator and account-page name
  • Date of death, last authorised use and post-death activity
  • Balance screen, transaction history and update date
  • Separate columns for balance, pending claims and family benefits
  • Applicant list, other heirs and any authority
  • Verified amount and approved payment route
  • Completion record, receipt and family reconciliation
