Hong Kong families and Mainland China estates

Hong Kong–Mainland estate checklist: Timeline Milestones

Track what has been established, what is missing and who must act next. Four useful milestones are identifying the people entitled to participate, verifying the estate, preparing documents for the specific Mainland process, and following acceptance through to delivery. These are reporting milestones, not a four-step guarantee or a completion timetable.

2026-09-11

1. Establish the family and succession position

Bring the death record, identity documents, marriage records and evidence of family relationships together. The list should not be limited to relatives currently helping with the application. A child who died earlier, an adoption, a remarriage or a minor heir may change the questions that must be investigated. Keep original spellings and record name variations separately rather than altering documents.

If a will exists, record its date, who holds the original, whether other versions are known and which assets it addresses. A copy does not settle validity or entitlement. The deliverable at this stage is a family chart and a gap list, distinguishing supported relationships, unresolved questions and objections. Do not fill a gap with an assumption just to mark the stage complete.

2. Verify the estate, not merely the clues

List each property, bank account, investment, equity interest and receivable separately. Record its location, registered holder, type of right and the date the information was checked. For property, investigate the deceased's share and any mortgage or seizure. A company-owned building is not automatically a shareholder's personal estate; a loan owed by the company to the deceased is different from an equity interest.

Management-fee receipts, tax records, messages and keys can point towards assets but do not by themselves establish current ownership. Mark entries verified, awaiting enquiry or disputed. Include known liabilities, outstanding rent and expenditure after death. An evidence-linked inventory is more useful than a single estimated estate value with no supporting records.

3. Prepare documents for a defined receiving process

Ask the actual bank, registry, notarial office or court what it requires before arranging certification, translation or signatures. Explain that the family is in Hong Kong, where each document originated, where the asset is located and whether the intended task is an enquiry, succession confirmation or transfer. Acceptance by one institution does not establish acceptance by another.

For Hong Kong notarial documents intended for Mainland use, check the applicable China-Appointed Attesting Officer and examination/transmission arrangements. Do not substitute the apostille route used for foreign documents. Whether an inheritance notarisation or another legal document is needed depends on the receiving procedure; there is no sound basis for presenting one notarisation route as mandatory for every property case.

Separate authority to enquire, submit papers and collect documents from authority to dispose of assets or receive money. A waiver of inheritance or distribution agreement needs an informed decision, not simply a signature appointment. This milestone is reached when purpose, version, signatory and receiving requirements align. Courier delivery is not evidence of substantive acceptance.

4. Follow acceptance through to actual delivery

Keep the acknowledgement, reference number, submission date and receiving contact. Record each request for additional material, why it is needed, who will obtain it and any confirmed deadline. An enquiry response, formal acceptance, completion of additional-document review and final completion are different states. Acceptance of a bank application does not mean funds can already be withdrawn.

Check the final output for each asset: updated registration information for property, relevant company records and required changes for equity, and transaction and receipt records for funds. Maintain an original-document handover list and estate accounts. If money will later be remitted to Hong Kong, source-of-funds and remittance requirements must be checked separately; completing succession formalities is not approval of a cross-border transfer.

Do not let the milestone order conceal an urgent problem

Evidence that an asset may be disposed of, unexplained rent collection or receipt of court papers may require attention before the file is complete. Preserve lawfully held evidence and dates, and obtain advice on preservation measures or dispute procedures. Do not enter the deceased's accounts without authority, sign in another person's name or take company seals.

Hong Kong administration and Mainland work can sometimes proceed in parallel, but they establish different matters. A dispute about heirs, a will or ownership may change the route. Distinguish waiting for an institution from waiting for a family decision: each requires a different follow-up.

Use a six-field progress record

For each task, record the issue, existing evidence or receipt, missing item, responsible person, confirmed deadline and next review date. Write unconfirmed where no deadline has been established. Each update should identify a new verifiable fact and its source, rather than repeat that the matter is being processed.

For an initial review, start with the family chart, asset inventory, gap list and existing acknowledgements. Identify the most urgent decision before sharing extensive personal documents. This is general information; entitlement, document use and procedure require review of the particular facts.
