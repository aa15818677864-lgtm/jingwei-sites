Can a Hong Kong Heir Retract a Waiver of a Mainland China Bank Inheritance?

A change of mind does not itself undo a written renunciation. Under Mainland China's succession rules, a court decides whether to recognise a retraction raised before the estate is dealt with or during litigation, based on the reasons given. A retraction after the estate has been dealt with is not recognised under the current judicial interpretation.

Published: 2026-11-17

Work out which document was signed

Ask for the signed original and identify its recipient, date and scope. A document renouncing inheritance has a different purpose from an authority to ask a bank for information or submit papers. If the wording is disputed, identify the version actually delivered to the Mainland notary, court or bank.

Build a timeline from the account holder's death to the declaration, any inheritance certificate or court step, bank payment and distribution among the heirs. The absence of a payment to one family member does not, by itself, show that the estate remains unadministered.

A pre-distribution retraction is not automatic

Mainland China's Civil Code requires an heir who renounces inheritance to make a written declaration after succession begins and before the estate is dealt with. The Supreme People's Court interpretation says that, before the estate is dealt with or during litigation, a court decides whether to recognise a retraction by considering the stated reasons.

If the other heirs disagree, preserve the signing history and communications and obtain advice on the Mainland dispute process. Do not assume that a second letter instructs the bank to disregard the first one. The bank's payment requirements and the family dispute are separate matters.

After the estate has been dealt with, identify the actual dispute

The judicial interpretation does not recognise a change of mind after the estate has been dealt with. A bank payment made under the operative inheritance documents is not reversed merely because an heir now wants a share.

An allegation that a signature was forged, a document altered, or an authority to act mistaken for a renunciation is a different factual issue. Examine the original and the receiving institution's file before describing such a claim as a simple retraction.

Documents to prepare

  • Original declaration and delivery evidence
  • Death and family relationship documents
  • Bank inquiry and payment records
  • Notarial, distribution and court papers
  • Specific reasons and evidence for any challenge
