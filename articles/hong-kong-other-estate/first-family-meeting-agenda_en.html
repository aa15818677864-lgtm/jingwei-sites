First Family Meeting on a Mainland Estate: Use an Agenda Before Discussing Shares

The first meeting is not meant to finish the estate. Its job is to show what is confirmed, what remains uncertain and exactly what each person must produce before the next review.

Last updated：2026-08-04

Three points to keep in view

  • Confirm participants and roles
  • Separate facts, clues and unknowns
  • Give every task an owner, output and deadline

The short answer: use six agenda items to separate facts, custody and next steps

Work through participants, the asset list, present control, urgent preservation, document tasks and the next review date. Do not begin with percentages, and do not treat meeting notes as authority to withdraw, transfer, sell or sign for someone else.

The useful output is a working record of confirmed facts, open questions, assignments, objections and dates. Distribution comes later, when the people, assets and authority are sufficiently clear.

A composite scenario: everyone wants to settle the estate in one call

A mother dies in Hong Kong, leaving a Foshan apartment and several bank clues. Her three children live in Hong Kong, the Mainland and overseas. An uncle in the Mainland holds the keys, an old passbook and the tenant's number. The eldest child opens the video call by proposing equal shares and asks everyone to approve that evening.

One sibling believes the asset list is incomplete; another simply wants speed. The immediate questions are not the percentages. They are who must participate, who now controls the home and rent, whether the bank clues are current, and whether the uncle is only safeguarding records or asserting another arrangement.

Identify who must participate and who is only helping

List the deceased, possible heirs, people named in a will, minors or anyone who may need representation. Keep contacts, translators, key holders and administrative helpers in a separate role column.

For a missing participant, record whether the issue is no contact, time zone, refusal or unconfirmed identity. Silence is not consent, and passing on a message does not create authority to decide for that person.

Use three statuses for the first asset list

Mark each item confirmed, clue only or unverified. Identify property by address and registration clue; keep bank and investment identifiers in a secure schedule; list rent, vehicles, shares and debts separately.

If someone remembers another investment, record the source, last known date and document that could confirm it. Do not value or divide a recollection as though it were a verified asset.

Separate temporary custody from ultimate ownership

Decide who will safeguard originals, keys, devices, rent records and property notices, and how the family can see the inventory. Custody prevents loss; it does not award the asset.

Record temporary restrictions as well: no change to rent instructions, no disposal of contents, no waiver drafted for signature and no login using the deceased's identity. Track necessary expenses with purpose, payer, amount and evidence.

Give every task one owner, one output and one date

Replace “the eldest will deal with the bank” with a precise output: who will obtain which account clue or written requirement, by what date, where it will be stored and when others can review it.

A task may have helpers, but it needs one accountable owner. If the receiving institution must first confirm its requirements, make that written response the output instead of preparing a speculative document set.

Minutes must capture decisions, unknowns and objections

Use separate columns for agreed working steps, missing information and express objections. Do not turn “no comment for now” into unanimous approval.

Circulate one dated version and invite factual corrections or recorded objections. Detached signature pages, partial chat screenshots and competing attachments create a later dispute about what, if anything, was agreed.

Do not force a conclusion when the safeguards are missing

Pause distribution if a minor or vulnerable person is involved, a will is specifically challenged, assets are being moved, an heir is unreachable, a third party asserts an interest or the inventory is plainly incomplete.

The meeting can still preserve records and assign inquiries. A majority vote cannot replace an absent person's decision or a required process. Define the issue, then decide whether investigation, professional help, mediation or a formal route is appropriate.

First-meeting checklist

  • Participants, absences and roles
  • Confirmed assets, clues and unknowns
  • Current custody of originals, keys, devices and rent
  • Temporary no-disposal steps
  • Owner, output and deadline for every task
  • Agreed, unresolved and objected items
  • Next review date and one controlled set of minutes
