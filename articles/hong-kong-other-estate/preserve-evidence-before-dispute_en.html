Other Mainland Estate Assets / Evidence preservation

A relative controls the passbook, title papers or rent: what to preserve in the first week

The first week's task is not to decide guilt in a family chat. It is to ensure that the property, accounts, rent and originals still have a traceable history when the family needs to investigate them.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Create an objective asset-and-control snapshot
  • Preserve complete conversations and source files
  • Request an account in writing without unauthorised access

When one relative holds every record

This composite example reflects recurring enquiries. A father left a Dongguan flat and bank account. His Hong Kong daughter learns that an aunt holds the title papers, passbook, phone and tenant contact and has continued collecting rent. The aunt says all money was used for funeral matters but provides no account.

The daughter wants to accuse her publicly and have someone guess the banking password. Both may create new problems. The better opening is to record who controlled each item, when control began, the current asset state and every traceable payment clue.

The short answer: create a verifiable factual snapshot before choosing enquiry, handover, preservation or recovery

List the flat, accounts, phone, documents, lease, keys and tenant. Record the last confirmed date, current controller and source of each fact. Preserve chats, transaction alerts, rent records and document images in their original context rather than keeping only cropped screenshots.

Then make a restrained written request for an inventory, post-death account and custody of originals. If there is a live sale, continuing withdrawals, account re-binding or a refusal to provide any information, assess formal enquiries, estate administration or preservation measures.

Day one: make a snapshot without accusations

For each item, record only objective details: address and condition, tenant contact, bank and last four digits, phone number, last sighting of the title paper or passbook, and current custodian. Mark hearsay as unverified.

Add a timeline around the death: last rent, withdrawal alert, changed lock, removal of papers or instruction to pay a new account. Establish sequence before using words such as theft or misappropriation.

Preserve electronic context and source devices

Keep the surrounding chat, account identity, dates and attachments. Preserve email headers and original attachments. Match screenshots of transfers or rent with statements, leases or receipts where possible. A cropped image loses context quickly.

Do not forward everything to a group and wipe the source device. Export backups, record how each file was obtained and preserve the original phone or computer. A dynamic page can be recorded through a complete lawful demonstration without entering another person's account.

Use a neutral written request for inventory and handover

Identify the deceased, known assets, originals requested, post-death income and expense account and proposed interim custody, with a reasonable response date. Focus on joint verification rather than demanding an admission.

Ask a continuing tenant to preserve the lease and payment history and to be cautious about unsupported payment changes. A new collection account must rest on actual authority, not one relative's oral instruction.

Do not cross the line while gathering evidence

Do not guess passwords, impersonate the deceased, remove another person's device, redirect rent or publish identities and accusations online. Those acts can create separate legal and evidential difficulties.

Do not pose as a buyer, creditor or official to provoke a response. Reliable evidence comes from authentic records with a clear origin, not a staged fragment.

Know when family verification must escalate

A pending sale or mortgage, continuing unusual transfers, instructions to pay rent to a private account, likely destruction of originals or a complete refusal to account may justify urgent assessment.

Define the goal: discover assets, stop disposition, obtain an account, recover originals or pursue money already moved. Each goal requires different evidence and procedure; an aggressive letter cannot solve all five at once.

First-week evidence pack

  • Asset, original and controller snapshot
  • Timeline around the death
  • Complete chats, emails and source transaction records
  • Neutral written inventory and handover request
  • Immediate sale, withdrawal, re-binding or destruction risk
