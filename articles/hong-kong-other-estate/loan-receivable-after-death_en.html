Money lent by the deceased is an estate asset: rebuild the loan evidence when no complete note exists

A transfer proves that money moved. It does not always prove a loan. The missing chain is why it was paid, whether repayment was agreed and how the parties later performed.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Prove agreement and payment as separate elements
  • Preserve full chats, payment notes and partial repayments
  • Appoint a coherent estate voice before pursuing the debtor

Are transfer screenshots enough to pursue RMB 3 million?

This composite example reflects recurring enquiries. After Mr Law dies in Hong Kong, his family finds three transfers totalling RMB 3 million to a Dongguan business associate. There is no signed loan note. Several WeChat messages say 'repay half by year end', and two small incoming payments are labelled 'interest'.

The recipient now says this was a joint investment, not a loan. Mr Law's two children separately demand different balances. The claim turns on the character and outstanding amount of the payments, not on adding the transfers once.

The short answer: a receivable can be inherited, but its existence and balance must first be proved

Build four layers: an agreement to borrow or repay, actual delivery, later acknowledgment or performance, and a calculation of outstanding principal and return. Transfers, chats, voice messages, payment notes, partial repayments, accounts and third-party documents belong on one timeline.

Then decide who represents or jointly manages this estate claim. Different heirs making inconsistent demands, granting extensions or receiving money can leave the debtor uncertain about valid performance and weaken an otherwise coherent file.

Layer one: show that the parties discussed a loan

Find the purpose, amount, repayment date, interest, receiving account and complete demand conversations. Phrases such as 'received' or 'deal with it at year end' have several possible meanings without context.

Separate investment, sale proceeds, payments made for someone else and ordinary family transfers. Do not classify every payment as a loan or select only favourable messages.

Layer two: reconcile every advance and repayment

Create a ledger with date, sending account, recipient, amount, reference and linked message. For cash or third-party payment, preserve withdrawals, receipts, instructions and source of funds.

Partial repayments and 'interest' references may be important, but test whether they relate to another transaction. Show principal, agreed return, payments received and the asserted balance separately.

Layer three: preserve the phone and audio, not just pictures

Keep the original device, account details, complete conversations, voice files and attachments. Screenshots should show identity, date and surrounding text. A transcript may help review important audio, but cannot replace the source file.

If the device is locked, do not break in or impersonate the deceased. Preserve its condition and assess a lawful extraction method. Cloud backups and the counterpart's or another participant's messages can also supply leads.

Layer four: establish one estate position and payment channel

Identify all heirs, any will and any estate administrator. Correspondence should consistently state the basis, provisional balance, safeguarded payment method and continuing reconciliation rather than allowing each relative to negotiate alone.

If one heir receives a partial payment, issue a receipt stating its purpose and enter it in the estate account. This avoids later assertions of full settlement or suspicion that money was handled privately.

Know when to send a demand and when to investigate first

A planned asset transfer, express denial or approaching deadline may justify prompt formal action. But an inflated letter sent before the payment character, amount or debtor identity is established may be counterproductive.

Build an evidence matrix showing support, contrary material and gaps for every required fact. Then choose a verification contact, repayment negotiation, formal demand or further proceeding.

Receivable evidence matrix

  • Purpose, amount and agreement to repay
  • Each advance and linked communication
  • Acknowledgment, interest or partial repayment
  • Outstanding principal and calculation
  • Person authorised to pursue and receive for the estate
