No account number? Build a usable map of a relative's Mainland bank clues

A missing account number is not the end of the search. Hong Kong families can first map banks, cities, income sources, recurring payments and old transfers without trying to access the deceased's accounts.

Last updated: 2026-08-18

Three practical moves

  • Group every clue by bank, product and likely account-opening city.
  • Work backwards from salary, pension, rent, dividends and recurring deductions.
  • Ask each institution what authority and documents it needs before preparing Hong Kong papers.

Start with the actual decision

The receiving institution will usually need a clear link between the applicant, the deceased and the product being queried. A disciplined clue map is more useful than a folder of unexplained screenshots.

Separate confirmed facts, open questions and the outcome the family or business wants. That allows the Mainland handler to decide whether investigation, document preparation, negotiation, registration or a formal procedure comes next.

The risk that most often complicates the matter

Do not guess passwords, log in as the deceased or move funds before authority is established; those actions can destroy the clean account snapshot the family needs.

Working checklist

  • Identity and death details
  • Possible bank or product
  • Income and recurring payment sources
  • Statements, messages and transfer records
  • Source and reliability of each clue
