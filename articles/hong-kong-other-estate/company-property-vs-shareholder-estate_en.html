Other Mainland Estate Assets / Company and personal property

Company-owned property is not the shareholder's estate: trace shares and receivables first

Owning 60% of a company does not let the family take 60% of every office, vehicle and cash balance. The company and the shareholder are separate property holders.

Last updated：2026-08-03

Three points to keep in view

  • Identify whether the company or individual owns each asset
  • Separate shares, shareholder loans and distributions
  • Do not confuse inheritance with taking control of company property

One company, three different estate claims

This composite example reflects recurring enquiries. Mr Hui held 60% of a Shenzhen company. The company owns an office, two vehicles and bank funds. Mr Hui also transferred RMB 2 million from his personal account to the company under unclear accounting entries. After his death in Hong Kong, the family asks to transfer 60% of the office to his wife.

The other shareholder objects and says the RMB 2 million was a capital contribution, not a loan. The estate enquiry has at least three lines: the shares, any amount genuinely owed by the company to Mr Hui, and any personal distribution already created but unpaid. The office itself remains company property.

The short answer: deal with the shareholder's rights before attempting to divide company assets

A company ordinarily continues to own its registered office, vehicles, accounts and equipment and uses them to meet company liabilities. The deceased's estate may include shares, a genuine company debt owed to him, an already declared but unpaid distribution, or another evidenced personal right.

Each requires different proof. Shares turn on registration, shareholder records, constitutional documents and contribution. A shareholder loan turns on purpose, accounts and repayment terms. A distribution turns on a valid company decision. A transfer of money or possession of a company seal does not convert company assets into a personal bank account.

Start with the registered owner and stop slicing physical assets by percentage

List the registered or contracting party for the office, vehicles, accounts, equipment, intellectual property and commercial contracts. Place company-owned items on the company schedule and individually owned items on the estate lead schedule.

A share percentage describes rights in the company, not direct co-ownership of each physical item. Without a valid company decision and transaction, the family cannot remove equipment or ask a registry to carve out 60% of an office.

Verify shares through records, constitution and contribution

Review corporate registration, the shareholder register, constitutional documents, transfers and contribution records. Existing documents may address succession, buyout or the exercise of rights after a shareholder's death.

Separate shareholder status from director, manager or legal-representative functions. Succession to economic rights does not automatically install the heir in every management role; operational authority requires its own process.

Classify the RMB 2 million by purpose

Review payment references, loan terms, company decisions, accounts, interest and repayments. An 'other payable' ledger entry is a clue but must correspond with the real transaction and source records.

A shareholder loan may be a company debt to the estate. Capital normally belongs in the ownership and capital relationship. An expense paid for the company should be reconciled item by item. The family cannot choose the classification that produces the largest estate.

Keep the business running without seizing seals or money

A shareholder's death can disrupt payroll, tax, contracts and payments. Inventory seals, online banking, books, contracts and necessary approval arrangements, with a documented handover and safeguards against unilateral control.

The family should not use the deceased's phone to approve company payments or move company cash into an estate account. The other shareholder likewise cannot transfer assets opportunistically. Business continuity and estate verification need separate accounts.

A complete plan answers both family and corporate questions

The family file decides who takes the shares or their value, any shareholder loan and distributions. The corporate file addresses the shareholder register, decisions, management roles, co-shareholder rights and continuity. They connect, but serve different purposes.

If the company is loss-making, in liquidation, litigating or heavily secured, share value may be far below the office's apparent market price. Review liabilities and operational restrictions before choosing one heir, joint holding or an exit.

Four company-estate schedules

  • Company-owned and individually owned assets
  • Shares, constitution, register and contribution
  • Shareholder loans, expenses and distributions
  • Seals, banking, books and operational handover
  • Company liabilities, security, litigation and share valuation
