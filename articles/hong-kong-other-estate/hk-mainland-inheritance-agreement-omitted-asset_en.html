An Omitted Asset After Signing a Mainland Estate Distribution Agreement

If a Hong Kong family discovers a Mainland China asset after signing a distribution agreement, the asset should not simply be added to the old percentages. First determine whether the agreement was intended to cover only listed items or the estate as a whole. Then verify whether the deceased owned the newly identified asset, whether it has already been dealt with, and whether the heirs agree on how to address it.

Published: 2026-10-23

Read the asset scope and reservation language

Locate the inventory, general wording about the estate, method for calculating shares, performance clauses, and signature date. If the agreement lists particular homes or accounts but says nothing about other property, whether the omitted item is covered may depend on the full text, information available when the parties signed, and their communications. Wording such as “all estate assets” or “assets not yet identified” should be read together with the schedules.

Confirm that each heir who needed to participate signed and had the relevant status or authority. Verify the listed assets as well: the registered owner, legal nature, amount, and current status. Family recollection alone may not establish that an item belongs to the estate.

Verify ownership before proposing a new distribution

Collect property registration information, bank records, contracts, contribution and payment evidence, and any marital property agreement. A home registered in one person’s name may not resolve every ownership question, and a bank account may include someone else’s funds. Confirm what belongs to the estate before discussing how to add it to the distribution.

Record when the asset was found, who had the information, whether it was previously disclosed, and whether anyone asked for further investigation before signing. These facts can help explain how the parties understood the agreement; one person’s statement that they “did not know” does not by itself determine the legal effect. Preserve the full agreement, attachments, messages, and meeting notes, not only the signature page.

Use a written supplement if the heirs agree

If the relevant heirs agree, a written supplement can identify the asset, set out each share, describe the Mainland steps, allocate agreed expenses, and state which terms of the original agreement continue to apply. Avoid handwritten additions without confirmation from all parties. Keep separate records for items already transferred and items still awaiting action; an institution may request different supporting papers.

If an heir says the original agreement already covers the item, another refuses to sign, or ownership is disputed, identify the issue: interpretation, inheritance shares, or whether the property belongs to the estate. Disputes involving Mainland assets should be assessed under Mainland China law and procedure. What to gather after finding an omitted asset

  • The complete agreement, schedules, inventory, and signed versions
  • Registration, account, contract, payment, and co-ownership records
  • Evidence of the deceased’s ownership, debts, or third-party interests
  • Information disclosed and communications before signing
  • Other heirs’ positions and the receiving Mainland institution’s requirements
