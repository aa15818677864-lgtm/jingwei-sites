Rent and Withdrawals Continued for Six Months: How to Reconstruct the Estate Account

Do not begin with one accusation or one net figure. Connect every receipt, withdrawal, transfer and cash expense after the death to its source record so the real gaps become visible.

Last updated：2026-08-04

Three points to keep in view

  • Build an opening snapshot at the date of death
  • Classify transactions line by line
  • Keep unexplained items visibly unresolved

The short answer: build a transaction ledger that links every line back to source evidence

Define the properties, accounts and months under review. For each transaction, record the date, payer or recipient, amount, account, person who acted, stated purpose, evidence and verification status. Separate estate income, necessary costs, family advances, distributions, transfers between estate accounts and unexplained items.

An unsupported withdrawal should remain unverified rather than being labelled theft. A statement that it paid a property bill is not enough by itself either. The opening position, period activity and closing balances must ultimately reconcile.

A composite scenario: six months of activity reduced to one total

A father owned a rented apartment in Dongguan. After his death, a cousin in the Mainland dealt with the tenant and kept the bank card. Six months later, the Hong Kong daughter sees partial statements showing monthly rent, several cash withdrawals and transfers. The cousin says the money covered the funeral, management fees, repairs and family travel, but provides only a total.

Subtracting the closing balance from six months of rent would miss the pre-existing balance, tenant deposit, transfers between accounts, cash spending and money that may never have belonged solely to the estate. A traceable ledger is needed before the family can identify the real discrepancies.

Define the review period and opening position

Use the date of death as the main cut-off. Record known bank balances, cash, accrued rent, tenant deposits, unpaid property charges and payments already in progress. If the earliest statement starts later, mark the opening balance as missing.

Do not ignore every pre-death transaction or assume every one is estate money. Isolate only specific questions, such as a withdrawal close to the death with no known purpose, then examine whether it was the deceased's spending, jointly owned money or another arrangement.

Obtain source records through a proper route

Preserve complete statements, tenancy documents, rent receipts, tenant payment records, property bills, repair orders, original transfer images and full message threads. Number each source so every ledger line points back to a document.

Possession of a card, phone or password is not continuing authority to log in or transact. Confirm who may request institutional records and in what capacity. Do not create new unauthorised activity while trying to reconstruct old activity.

Use six transaction classes to avoid double counting

Separate estate receipts, necessary administration costs, family advances, distributions, transfers between estate accounts and unexplained transactions. Rent, returned deposits, interest and repayment of a debt are different sources and deserve separate lines.

A transfer from account A to account B is only an internal movement if both accounts are in scope. It should not be counted once as a loss and again as new income. Link a family member's advance to any later reimbursement as well.

Build a destination trail for cash withdrawals

For each cash withdrawal, identify who acted, when, the stated purpose and the documents or witnesses that can support it. Receipt dates, amounts and services should reasonably connect with the withdrawal.

A receipt may show that a payment occurred without proving that this particular cash funded it. Use partly verified or unverified where the chain is incomplete; do not invent a purpose to make the spreadsheet balance.

Ask neutral, answerable questions

Turn each gap into one question: which account received the March rent, who made this withdrawal, what part of the property was repaired, where is the original invoice, and who now holds the remaining funds? Give a reasonable response date.

Keep the earlier version when new records arrive and note the update date and source. The remaining disagreement will then be identifiable as amount, purpose, authority or authenticity rather than a general accusation.

Act on continuing risk before the ledger is perfect

If withdrawals continue, rent instructions are changing, property is being sold, records are being destroyed or all accounting is refused, preserve the current statements, tenant details and communications and assess how to stop further loss.

Reconstruction is not a private verdict or a distribution. Where ownership, the deceased's share, authority or transaction authenticity is seriously disputed, give the verified ledger and source records to a professional for the next-step assessment.

Minimum fields for the estate ledger

  • Review period and opening snapshot
  • Date, amount, payer and recipient
  • Account and person who acted
  • Receipt, cost, advance, distribution, internal transfer or unknown
  • Purpose and source-document number
  • Verified, partly verified or unverified
  • Closing balance and next missing record
