Mainland estates for Hong Kong families

Several wills cover Mainland assets: compare dates, formalities and conflicting clauses

When a family finds several wills, the newest or notarised document should not be selected without review. Compare each date, form, signature, witnesses, capacity and property scope, then assess conflicting clauses under the applicable law.

Published: 2026-10-24

Preserve each original and its creation history

Keep originals, envelopes, drafts, edits, witness details, recordings and custody records. A scan is not a substitute for an original. Record when and where each document was found; do not annotate or staple originals.

Test formal validity and genuine intent

Handwritten, witnessed, printed, audio and video wills have different formal requirements. For printed or dictated documents, verify witness eligibility, presence throughout, signatures and date. Also consider capacity, coercion allegations and whether the will covers the testator’s own property.

Resolve only the portions that actually conflict

The Mainland Civil Code provides that where several wills conflict, the later will prevails. First establish that each is valid and covers the same property. For a Hong Kong document or cross-border succession, separately assess form and governing law rather than relying on date alone.

Documents and points to check

  • Originals, dates and custody history for every will.
  • Form, signatures, witnesses and recordings.
  • Capacity and any evidence of a dispute.
  • Property covered and clauses that conflict.
  • Place of execution, asset location and governing-law questions.
