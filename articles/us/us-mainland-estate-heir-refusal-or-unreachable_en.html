Mainland estates for U.S. families

A U.S.-Based Heir Refuses to Sign or Cannot Be Reached

When a family member in the United States has not signed papers for an inheritance in Mainland China, first determine what has happened. The heir may reject a proposed distribution, refuse one document, have not replied yet, or be genuinely unreachable. These situations should not be labelled “renunciation.” A person does not automatically lose inheritance rights because the family cannot reach them, and the other heirs should not distribute that person’s share without addressing the issue.

Published: 2026-10-23

The heir is reachable but disagrees

Preserve the full communications and identify the dispute: the assets included, the list of heirs, a will, proposed shares, or how a property should be handled. Repeated requests for a signature do not resolve a substantive disagreement. Once the records are clearer, separate matters the heirs agree on from items that need investigation or cannot presently be settled.

If agreement is not possible, assess the dispute procedure in Mainland China and confirm how the relevant heirs should participate, where notices should be sent, and which identity or relationship records from abroad may be required. An heir’s residence in the United States does not itself replace Mainland steps relating to assets there. This article addresses the Mainland part only.

No response or address unknown

Make a dated record of the last contact, known telephone numbers and email addresses, addresses provided by relatives, returned mail, and each attempt to reach the person. An old address is different from uncertainty about the person’s identity. Confirm basic facts, including whether the person is alive. The family’s search record may explain what was tried, but it does not mean that a court has declared the person missing or deceased.

If the heir is genuinely unreachable, the Mainland procedure may require attention to notice, service, or another legal issue. The competent authority must determine the appropriate method, including whether a public notice or application is needed. Do not assume informal family efforts replace procedural requirements.

Refusal to sign is not formal renunciation

An heir may refuse to sign a distribution agreement because they disagree with its terms. That does not necessarily mean they have renounced an inheritance. Renunciation requires the heir’s own clear statement and compliance with applicable procedure. Relatives should not sign for an unreachable heir, make a renunciation in that person’s name, or allocate the share in advance.

For cross-border papers, ask the Mainland receiving institution what form and proof requirements apply, and keep dispatch, delivery, and returned-mail records. This is general procedural information, not a prediction of a particular outcome. Jingwei Law handles Mainland China legal matters only and does not provide U.S. local legal services.

Before choosing a path

  • The heir’s identity and all known contact details
  • Whether the person disagrees, refuses one paper, has not replied, or is unreachable
  • Dates and records of contact attempts and returned mail
  • The asset list, any will, and other heirs’ positions
  • Mainland notice, service, and foreign-document requirements
