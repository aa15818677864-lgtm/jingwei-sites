Article / U.S. families and Mainland loan guarantees

A deceased person guaranteed a Mainland loan: separate the creditor, borrower and guarantee issues

If a U.S.-based heir discovers that the deceased signed a guarantee for a Mainland loan or company financing, do not ask only whether “the money is part of the estate.” Separate whether the deceased was the lender, borrower or guarantor, and whether the obligation is due, being collected, litigated or secured. The estate may hold a receivable, owe a debt, or face a contingent liability.

First question to settle

Preserve the loan and guarantee agreements, payment records, repayment history, demand letters and court papers. Put signing and death dates on one timeline. Do not admit a debt, waive collateral or promise repayment before the representative and facts are confirmed. See Mainland court papers and estate debts.
