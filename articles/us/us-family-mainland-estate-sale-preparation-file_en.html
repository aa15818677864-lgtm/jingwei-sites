Mainland estates for U.S. families

Preparing to sell a Mainland estate property while living in the US: documents to gather before registration

A family can organise sale-related records before inheritance registration is complete. That preparation should be kept separate from commitments to a buyer. For a relative living in the United States, the useful starting point is a reliable Mainland property file and a clear account of who may act.

Published: 2026-10-23

Separate inherited rights from a completed transfer

The Mainland Civil Code distinguishes acquisition through inheritance from the registration required for a subsequent disposition. An unchanged certificate does not mean that inheritance rights cannot exist. Equally, an heir's entitlement does not mean a buyer's registered ownership can immediately be completed. Confirm the required sequence with the registry where the property is located.

List potential heirs, existing co-owners, wills and completed notarial or court procedures. Establish who may instruct an agent, decide the price, sign and receive money. Agreement from the relative coordinating the family is not automatically authority from every relevant rights holder.

Gather records before making a sale promise

Collect the original purchase agreement, invoices, available tax records, ownership documents, current registry information and management or maintenance records. Historical acquisition evidence may matter in the eventual tax review. If something is missing, ask what replacement evidence is acceptable rather than quoting an online tax figure as the final cost.

Make a separate schedule for any loan, mortgage, tenancy, deposit and actual occupant. Identify who holds the keys, what items remain inside and what would be needed for vacant possession. A tenanted sale also requires attention to the tenant's relevant rights and notification. Do not describe the home as unencumbered or immediately available for delivery without checking these matters.

Review documents and authority before signing

An agency instruction, letter of intent or deposit arrangement can contain binding provisions. Its title does not determine its effect. Review seller capacity, conditions for performance, incomplete inheritance work, custody of funds, delivery and default provisions. A buyer's payment should not run ahead of an unexplained registration obstacle.

If a US-based family member appoints a Mainland representative, confirm the receiving institution's documentary requirements and define enquiry, inheritance, sale and receipt powers separately. Recheck the applicable Mainland tax, banking and registration requirements when the transaction is ready. Overseas residence does not replace the Mainland ownership and authority checks.

Six preparation files

  • Heirs, co-owners and existing legal documents.
  • Purchase, invoice and historical tax records.
  • Ownership documents and current registry search.
  • Loan, tenancy, deposit and occupation schedule.
  • Delivery conditions, outstanding charges and contents.
  • Agency powers, contract conditions and institutional requirements.
