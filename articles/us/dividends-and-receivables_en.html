Mainland Company Money: Dividend, Loan or Receivable?

Do not label every company-related payment as “share proceeds.” A bank transfer shows that money moved; it does not, by itself, show whether the payment was a dividend, loan repayment, reimbursement, salary, capital contribution or personal advance.

Last updated: 2026-07-28

Four provisional buckets

  • Distribution record but no payment: dividend pending
  • Company received personal funds: company loan pending
  • Another person acknowledged the balance: personal receivable pending
  • The deceased owed the company: estate liability pending

The practical answer

Start with a claim map. For each amount, identify the person or entity said to owe it, the person said to receive it, the reason, the relevant date and the supporting record. Use “classification pending” where the records do not yet agree.

This exercise also catches amounts moving in the opposite direction. Money the deceased owed the company may be an estate liability, not an estate receivable. Keep it out of the asset total until the direction has been checked.

Unpaid dividends require more than evidence of profit

Confirm that the deceased was a shareholder at the relevant time, then look for the specific distribution record, the amount allocated, any conditions or deductions, and the payment history. Company profit alone does not establish an immediately payable dividend.

If the bank statement shows a company payment, compare the memo, company record, accounting description and any payments made to other shareholders at the same time. Whether a payment right had actually arisen still depends on the underlying documents and the handling of the company where it is registered.

A loan to the company needs a complete transaction chain

Where the deceased transferred personal funds to the company, record the recipient account, stated purpose, any loan or balance confirmation, maturity date, interest terms and repayments. A transfer image alone may also fit a capital contribution, expense advance or other arrangement.

Put every outgoing and returning payment on one timeline. Reconcile the original amount with repayments and offsets. If the company ledger and bank records differ, show the gap rather than carrying the first transfer forward as the current balance.

A personal receivable follows the actual debtor

The deceased may have advanced money to another shareholder, employee, customer or relative. A conversation in a company chat or a payment through a company-linked personal account does not automatically make the company the debtor.

A useful row might read: “March 2025 / payment to Zhang / memo says temporary advance / Zhang later acknowledged RMB 200,000 outstanding / personal loan classification pending.” Preserve the acknowledgement and payment route as separate evidence.

Check whether the deceased owed money back to the company

A ledger label such as “shareholder loan” can point either way. If the deceased took company funds, collected money without remitting it, or had an outstanding advance, the amount may be a liability requiring estate review.

Ask for the sub-ledger and source documents when the account uses only a net figure, unclear positive and negative signs, or years of offsets. The family should neither reverse the direction nor accept the company’s full figure before the entries are traced.

Public company information identifies the entity, not the transaction

Save the company’s full registered name, city, current status and disclosed shareholder or contribution information. This is a useful identity check, but it does not replace distribution records, loan confirmations, ledgers or bank evidence for a particular amount.

Keep three folders: company and share identity, transaction evidence, and U.S. death and estate-authority papers. The company’s own cash, property and equipment remain company assets; they are not divided into the deceased shareholder’s personal estate by percentage.

Identify who may act for the U.S. estate

Being named executor in a will is not always the same as being appointed by a court. Record whether the family has a will nomination, a pending application or issued authority. A beneficiary, family coordinator and appointed personal representative may have different standing when requesting records or payment.

The family can preserve evidence before appointment, but company money should not be routed to a relative’s personal account merely for convenience. Confirm the U.S. estate-administration step, the Mainland company’s review and the final payment route separately.

Close the file with a seven-point check

Use four provisional buckets: distribution record but no payment, “dividend pending”; company received the deceased’s personal funds, “company loan pending”; another person acknowledged the balance, “personal receivable pending”; company says the deceased owed money, “estate liability pending.” Finalise a label only when direction, amount and evidence align.

Checklist:
• Full company name, registered city and current status
• Creditor, debtor, reason, date and amount for each entry
• Distribution record, loan terms, balance confirmation or acknowledgement
• Bank statement, payment memo, repayments and claimed outstanding balance
• Separate records for shares, company offices and personal dealings
• U.S. representative status: nominated, applying or appointed
• The next person or institution that can resolve each open point

If the company denies the transaction, another shareholder controls the books, figures have changed, money moved to an affiliate, or relatives dispute who may receive it, preserve the originals and timeline. The problem is no longer an accounting label; it is proof of each claim, liability and representative role.
