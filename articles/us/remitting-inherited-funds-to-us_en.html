Sending a Mainland Inheritance to the U.S.: Keep the Money Trail

Build one file that shows where the money came from, why it belongs to you and how it reached the United States.

Last updated: 2026-07-25

The file should answer three questions

  • What was the original asset?
  • Why did this heir receive it?
  • How did the renminbi become a U.S. bank credit?

Do not keep only the final wire receipt

The record should begin with the original asset, connect it to the death and inheritance papers, and then follow any sale or withdrawal through currency conversion, remittance and the U.S. bank credit. A final wire receipt rarely shows whether the money began as a home, deposit or another estate asset.

Before the remittance, confirm the route with the bank or receiving authority based on the heir's status and identity documents, the deceased's former household registration and any representative involved. Do not split the funds into smaller transfers to avoid review.

1. Preserve the original asset record

For sale proceeds, keep the original property record, sale contract, payment history and relevant tax or fee documents. For a bank deposit, keep the account or product clue, collection record and bank papers. The final renminbi balance is not a substitute for the source documents.

2. Show why this heir received the money

Keep the death record, family or heirship papers, will or distribution material, and the record showing who received each share. If several heirs are involved, the file should identify whose money is being sent. If a representative acts, retain the authority, identity and each movement of funds.

3. Follow every step from renminbi to the wire

Keep the Mainland application, review, currency-conversion and outgoing-remittance records, including sender, recipient, currency, amount, date and stated purpose. Do not describe funds from different sources simply as a family transfer. Add a name-matching sheet where English, Chinese or former identity documents differ.

4. Keep the file after the U.S. bank receives the money

Save the U.S. bank credit, dollar amount, exchange rate and sender details. Some U.S. persons receiving a large foreign bequest, or funds connected with a foreign trust, may have an information-reporting question. Form 3520 is one such information return; it is not a wire form and does not apply automatically to every inheritance.

Reporting depends on the recipient, source, amount and tax year. A successful bank credit does not resolve the U.S. reporting analysis. Give the complete money trail to a U.S. tax professional familiar with cross-border estates.
