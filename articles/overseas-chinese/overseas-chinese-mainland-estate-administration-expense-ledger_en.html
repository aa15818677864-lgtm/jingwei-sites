Mainland estates for families abroad

Keeping an Expense Ledger for a Mainland Estate from Overseas

When relatives outside Mainland China help preserve or administer an estate there, expenses can become hard to explain. A family chat may say only that someone has “paid,” without identifying the asset, purpose, decision-maker, or whether reimbursement is being requested. A clear ledger makes those issues visible. Whether an expense may be paid from estate property depends on the applicable law, the management arrangement, and the facts; a receipt alone does not decide the question.

Published: 2026-10-23

Use one ledger for everyone handling the estate

Record each transaction by date, description, purpose, amount, payer, recipient, payment method, related Mainland asset, and location of the supporting record. Keep property charges, necessary repairs, document costs, safekeeping expenses, and personal travel costs in separate categories. Do not combine them under a broad label such as “inheritance expenses.” Consistent bookkeeping does not mean every category is reimbursable from estate property.

If several relatives are involved, one person can maintain the ledger, while others confirm major or non-urgent decisions. A relative who pays first has not necessarily obtained agreement that the estate should bear the amount. For urgent maintenance or preservation, record when the issue was discovered, whom the payer notified, and when payment was made.

Connect each payment to an estate need

An invoice or bank record shows that money moved; it may not show why the expense related to the estate. Add a short explanation. For a repair, keep the quotation, work order, and photographs; for a property or utility charge, keep the bill and payment confirmation; for a document service, record what was done, which institution handled it, and what was received.

When asking another heir to contribute, state whether the request concerns proof of payment, who should bear the cost, or whether it may be paid from estate assets. Those are separate questions. If an amount or purpose is disputed, retain the original records and full transaction history, and list the disputed item separately from expenses no one contests.

Separate administration from an advance by a relative

The Civil Code sets out general responsibilities for estate administrators, but whether a particular expense may be drawn from an estate depends on the arrangement, the asset, necessity, and any agreement among heirs. An administrator should keep records that heirs can check. If the administrator also claims compensation or provides a paid service, list the basis, recipient, and calculation separately.

This article concerns accounting for estates involving Mainland China. It does not provide local legal services where an overseas heir lives. Jingwei Law handles Mainland China legal matters only. If heirs dispute an expense or distribution, preserve the original ledger and communications and address the issue through the appropriate Mainland procedure.

Records for every expense

  • Date, amount, payer, recipient, and payment method
  • Purpose and the Mainland estate asset involved
  • Invoice, receipt, quotation, photograph, or bank record
  • Prior approval, urgency, and notice to other heirs
  • Whether the item is an advance, contribution request, or proposed estate payment
