Mainland estates for families abroad

Mapping inherited and pre-existing shares in a Mainland co-owned home: a guide for overseas families

Two names on a Mainland property certificate do not mean that the survivor automatically receives the whole home. Nor should every two-person ownership be assumed to be equal. Build an evidence chart showing the original interests before tracing what passes through inheritance.

Published: 2026-10-23

Identify the original form of co-ownership

Read the register alongside the acquisition contract and effective agreements. Establish whether ownership is in defined shares or common ownership, and whether proportions were agreed. Rules for unrelated investors should not simply be applied to a family relationship. Where defined shares are unclear, the applicable rules and contribution evidence need examination.

The Mainland rule separating half of marital common property for the surviving spouse applies subject to other agreements. It is not a rule that every property held by two people is owned equally. Premarital acquisition, gifts and other valid property arrangements may require a different analysis. Paying instalments or living in the home does not automatically create a matching ownership percentage.

Give each succession its own layer

The first layer should identify original owners, interests and supporting documents. The next records the part belonging to a particular deceased person, followed by the will, relevant heirs and other applicable arrangements. Keep a surviving co-owner's pre-existing property outside the deceased's estate.

Where relatives died at different times, work through each death with the family relationships then existing. Someone may first inherit an interest and later die, leaving that interest relevant to a further succession. Mark the evidence, gaps and disputed assumptions at every layer. Jumping straight to a final fraction can conceal a missing heir or an unresolved intermediate interest.

Ownership proportions do not settle the practical division

An established percentage does not mean the apartment can physically be divided into matching rooms. If one person wants to stay and another wants cash, review the co-ownership agreement, legal conditions for partition and practical options. A negotiated buyout or lawful division of proceeds may be considered; unresolved disputes require assessment of the actual ownership structure and evidence.

Record occupation, document custody, necessary expenses and representation separately. Reimbursement claims and ownership percentages should not be silently combined. Before a sale or family buyout is finalised, verify the relevant rights holders, disposal authority and payment conditions. One coordinating relative's promise is not a substitute for an effective arrangement involving those entitled to act.

Documents behind the share chart

  • Current register and historical ownership records.
  • Acquisition, co-ownership and contribution evidence.
  • Each death date and corresponding relationship evidence.
  • Wills and existing notarial or effective court documents.
  • Layered interests, evidence gaps and disputed assumptions.
  • Occupation, advances and agency records.
