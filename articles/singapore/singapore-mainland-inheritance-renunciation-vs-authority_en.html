Renouncing a Mainland Inheritance or Authorising a Signature from Singapore?

Renunciation and authorisation serve different purposes. A renunciation is the heir’s statement that they will not accept an inheritance right. An authorisation appoints someone to carry out specified steps or sign within defined limits. An heir who is overseas and cannot attend a Mainland procedure should not be treated as having renounced merely because a relative is asked to handle paperwork.

Published: 2026-10-23

Choose between giving up a right and delegating a task

If the heir wants to preserve their rights but cannot travel to Mainland China, ask the receiving institution which steps may be delegated, what the authority must cover, and how an overseas-signed paper may be used. An agent may be authorised to file or collect documents, communicate with an institution, or sign a specified document. A broad instruction to “handle the inheritance” should not be treated as proof that the principal has given up a share.

If the heir has decided not to accept an inheritance, the intention should be stated clearly and the applicable timing and form requirements checked. Renunciation does not automatically transfer the share to a person chosen by the heir. Nor does it give an agent discretion to decide the distribution. A will, estate debts, and an agreement among heirs raise separate questions.

Read the operative wording, not only the title

Inheritance papers may ask a person to confirm family relationships, whether a will exists, the assets involved, or a proposed distribution. Read every page and look for language such as “renounce,” “waive,” “receive,” “transfer,” or “authorise disposal.” A distribution agreement records how the heirs divide property; it is not simply permission for someone else to deliver forms.

Before signing in Singapore, obtain the complete document and attachments instead of relying on a relative’s summary or a partly completed form. Check the property and heirs involved and whether any right is being given up. Then ask the Mainland institution what execution, certification, or transfer formalities it requires for an overseas-signed document.

Keep the two intentions in separate records

If the heir both appoints an agent for procedural tasks and intends to renounce a right, record those intentions in separate, clear documents. Do not use “full authority” as a substitute for either. Keep the signed versions, communications, dispatch records, and receipt confirmations. If the signer later sees a mismatch between the text and their understanding, preserve the original and the communications explaining the intention.

Whether a statement or authority is sufficient depends on the complete text, the estate, and the receiving institution’s current requirements.

Before signing

  • Does the heir want to retain the right and delegate tasks, or to renounce?
  • Does the text cover distribution, receipt of funds, or disposal of property?
  • Has the signer reviewed the complete document and attachments?
  • What formalities does the Mainland institution require for overseas execution?
  • Are the authority and renunciation recorded separately?
