Preserve verifiable records before choosing a route

Singapore estate administration and the handling of a Mainland China asset answer different questions. Put the source, date, people and asset clues in order before deciding what the record proves and what remains missing.

Until authority and facts are checked, do not log into accounts, sign for someone else, dispose of an asset or promise a transfer, recovery or distribution outcome.

1. Preserve the original source

Keep the original, full screen or document, envelope, attachments and the date obtained; a forwarded excerpt usually loses important context.

For another person's account, device or private material, use only lawfully held records. Do not guess passwords or bypass a verification step.

2. Separate people, assets and dates

Use one sheet to record whom the material names, which Mainland city or asset it points to, and the date and source for each item.

If names or dates do not match, mark them for checking rather than rewriting them or assuming they identify the same person.

3. Ask one specific Mainland question

Tell the receiving side what record exists, where the asset is, what is known about estate authority and exactly what needs confirmation.

Requirements can differ with the asset type, city and family situation; obtain a direction before preparing more papers.

4. Keep the next steps in a checklist

Separate originals held, copies to obtain, people to verify, institutions to contact and any deadline.

If there is a dispute, suspected alteration or an urgent deadline, preserve the materials first and obtain advice on the appropriate Mainland route.
